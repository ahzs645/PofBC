# Forest Closure Zone 3: reconstruction notes

## Source and date

Both supplied photographs appear together on printed page 4 of the British Columbia Forest Service's *Forest Service Protection Bulletin*, No. 3, 1960, under the heading "Forest Closure Zone Signs." The accompanying account describes twelve pilot highway signs whose lower panel could be changed by the local ranger between OPEN and WOODS CLOSED TO TRAVEL. The bulletin does not name a typeface, sign manufacturer, paint colour, or physical plate dimensions. Its 1960 issue date is established; the JUL 5 1960 library stamp should not be treated as a more precise publication date. [1]

The government PDF contains the same photographic evidence as the supplied crops. Its scanned page provides provenance and surrounding context, but does not resolve substantially more detail in the tiny oval emblem.

## How the artwork was rebuilt

The reconstruction uses a reusable lettering system and clean geometric components. Main letterforms are independently constructed from deliberate straight segments, curves, counters, and shared proportions. Repeated letters use the same master shape. The triangle, inner border, and supplemental panel are separate vector components, and the lettering remains individually editable as paths. The accompanying reconstruction font contains the characters needed for these two signs; it is a specimen subset, not a complete historical alphabet.

Photographic grain, scratches, blurred edges, and small painted irregularities are treated as uncertainty in the evidence. They are not reproduced as hundreds of contour-following nodes. The result is a coherent, scalable interpretation of the sign design. It should be described as a reconstruction, rather than an original government production master.

## Font finding

**The strongest defensible identification is heavy geometric sans-serif sign lettering in the Futura/Spartan tradition. The exact historical font is unverified.**

The photographs show a flat-topped A, a conventional steep N diagonal, heavy unequal B bowls, an almost circular outer O with a taller counter, and a relatively short R leg. The numeral 3 is especially diagnostic: it has broad, unequal rounded bowls, sloping left terminals, and an extended open waist. An unchanged modern geometric font does not reproduce all of these details together. This finding is an interpretation of the two supplied photographs, not a documented original font attribution.

League Spartan and Jost provide useful modern comparison controls, but neither should be presented as the original 1960 face. League Spartan's creators describe its relationship to ATF Spartan and its own design changes. Jost's creator explicitly distinguishes its Futura-inspired approach from an exact reproduction. Bauer's documentation also distinguishes the original Futura family from its later digital versions. [2-4]

The reconstruction therefore uses source-informed shared masters, including a separately constructed 3. OPEN has a wider setting, while WOODS CLOSED TO TRAVEL uses a narrower setting with a visibly smaller TO. Independent width and height scaling accounts for the principal proportional changes. Locally adjusted O/N counters and diagonals improve the fitted reconstruction, but do not establish a different historical font. P appears only in OPEN, so it cannot distinguish two source alphabets. Neither an identical commercial font nor different commercial fonts can be proved from these scans.

## What the overlap comparison establishes

The two photographs are registered to a common geometric convention before comparing lettering. The supplemental panel is treated separately because it can hang in a different plane from the triangular plate. The ideal equilateral triangle, concentric inner border, and normalized width are reconstruction assumptions; they are not dimensions measured from a surviving drawing.

Overlap scores describe agreement between a rendered reconstruction and a specified photographic mask under the reported alignment and threshold choices. Scores from individually resized glyphs describe shape compatibility with letter width and spacing removed. Scores from assembled words or complete sign regions additionally reflect placement. Those different scores should not be presented as interchangeable.

A high overlap does not prove the original font's name. Where the reconstruction was adjusted using the same photograph that was scored, the result is a fitted comparison. Comparison against the second photograph provides a separate useful check, while differences in blur, perspective, print reproduction, and painted edges limit how literally small percentage changes can be interpreted. The report's metric tables and overlay captions identify the applicable comparison.

## The Forest Service oval

The ring wording is legible: FOREST SERVICE above and BRITISH COLUMBIA below. The sparse, tall tree with its exposed lower trunk is a feature of the supplied sign. The small emblem has been rebuilt with mathematical ovals, individually outlined lettering, a manually drawn tree, and a restrained landscape. Its fine branch forms and micro-lettering are approximate.

A Forest Service historical account documents numerous drawn variants. Its Figure 8 shows the same top-and-bottom wording arrangement in a 1933 example; Figure 17 shows a sparse-tree oval on a 1960s wharf sign. These support the historical structure used here without establishing an exact match to the tiny sign decal. [5]

The emblem's micro-lettering uses condensed Nimbus Sans Bold outlines as a readable approximation. The emblem should be excluded from claims about exact main-font matching. A sharper period decal, drawing, or close photograph would be needed to substantiate its smallest details.

## Size, colour, and confidence

The files use normalized design units and can be scaled. Both status faces now share a 1000 x 130 rectangle. The earlier CLOSED geometry mistakenly extended to the photograph boundary; the actual lower edge is around source-image y1020-1028 and is consistent with the OPEN strip. The same reversible or interchangeable strip design is strongly supported, while the exact mechanism and manufactured dimensions remain unverified. No physical width, height, or production tolerance is established by the photographs.

The photographs are grayscale. The black-and-white output preserves their tonal organization: dark lettering on the light main plate and OPEN panel, and light lettering on the dark CLOSED panel. It does not establish whether the original dark material was black, green, or another colour, or specify original paint or reflectivity.
