# Legislation, orders and policy documents

The instrument catalogue (`src/government/instrumentsData.js`) is generated by
`scripts/build-government-instruments.mjs` from evidence already in the repository. A
relationship is only what its evidence says:

| Relationship | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `established_by` | The instrument created the body (on the date the evidence gives). |
| `continued_under` | An existing body continued under a later instrument. |
| `reorganized_by` | Its structure, name, status, area or responsibility changed. |
| `ended_by` | The instrument ended or dissolved it. |
| `administers` | A ministry was charged with administering the Act. |
| `current_legal_basis` | What the current consolidation cites as its basis: today's footing, never a founding date. |
| `announced` | A policy document announced by a ministry. |

## How citations are read

1. A sub-agency's `legalBasis`, as its current legal basis.
2. "designated by M###/####, effective that day" in a body's `establishedMeaning`.
3. Relationships checked against an instrument's own text (`relations` in the decisions file).
4. Events citing BC Laws: the one instrument named by the event's BC Laws source (when it is that
   document's own citation), else by its title, else by its locator. The event type decides the
   relationship. Ministries are only renamed by their events: orders filed under a ministry about
   bodies the diagram does not draw are dated events citing the instrument, not relationships.

Anything else goes to `research/audit/instruments-review.json`.

## Decisions

`instrument-decisions.json` records reviewed readings of citations the rules cannot read safely,
each with its reason: per event, per body, per legal-basis citation, Act aliases (one Act cited two
ways) and Acts reviewed as distinct enactments. A decision's `cite` must read as exactly one
instrument, so IDs stay stable. Adding a decision is editing, not research: it uses what the event's
title and locator, or the body's record, already say.

The review list is kept empty (`scripts/instruments.test.mjs`): a new citation the rules cannot read
fails the test until it is decided.

## The first pilot: B.C. Reg. 293/2001

The Regional Health Boards Regulation (M297/2001, deposited 12 December 2001) was checked against its
text: s. 2 amalgamates the 44 authorities in Schedule A, column 2 into the five in column 1, and
s. 4(1) continues those five as regional health boards. Every column 2 name matches a body whose record
already ends that day and becomes the column 1 authority.
