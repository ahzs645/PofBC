# B.C. political party logos, names and changes

Prepared **22 November 2026** for the PofBC project.

## Start here

1. Extract the ZIP completely.
2. Open **index.html** in a browser for the searchable offline gallery. No installation or internet connection is required to browse the included images. Source links open the original online evidence.
3. Open **BC_Political_Party_Logo_Catalogue.pdf** for the illustrated historical catalogue.
4. Use **assets/** for the recovered artwork and **data/assets.csv** or **data/assets.json** for filenames, source dates, attribution and checksums.

## Contents

- **65 catalogued source-artwork records**, covering 12 identities with recovered artwork.
- **3 companion cropped PDF files**, where a source extraction also has a vector/PDF version.
- **27 party, predecessor or historical-label coverage records**, including transparent gaps.
- **86 referenced sources** and **27 organizational/name/history records**, alongside separate logo-specimen observations.
- **previews/** contains display derivatives; these are not counted as additional recovered logos.
- **data/events.csv/json** distinguishes formation, renaming, splitting, campaign withdrawal, caucus changes, registration changes and observed artwork.
- **data/coverage.csv/json** identifies missing artwork and incomplete periods.
- **data/sources.csv/json** is the bibliography; source IDs are shared with the PDF and gallery.

The counts include colour variants and file formats. They are not counts of unique historical redesigns. Three plain-name crops and campaign/leader treatments are identified in the records instead of being promoted to standardized institutional logos.

## Scope

The collection follows the governing, Official Opposition and other historically MLA-represented provincial parties discussed in the PofBC audit. It also records relevant predecessor names, coalition and independent alignments. The earliest recovered name specimens are from 1923–1924; the roster reaches back to the pre-1903 alignment context. Each historical identity without an authenticated image has a coverage record.

## Reading the dates

- **Dated use:** a newspaper advertisement or party publication shows the mark on a specific date. This establishes use, not the first or final use.
- **File observation:** a Commons upload, Drive timestamp, filename year or retrieval date describes the recovered file. It is not automatically the adoption date of a design.
- **Political event:** a party formation, legal name change, coalition or MLA move is recorded independently of its logo.
- **Unknown:** gaps are left explicit. No historical logos were traced, invented or generated to fill them.

For example, a 2017 BC NDP advertisement already shows the italic sun-box design supplied in current files named “2023”. The upright 2017 Commons vector does not establish a later replacement. Similarly, CentreBC's 2025 and 2026 specimens show differences without establishing exact changeover dates.

## Names and relationships

- **BC Liberal → BC United:** registered name effective 29 March 2023; public launch 12 April 2023. The 2024 campaign withdrawal is not recorded as a formal merger with the Conservatives.
- **CCF → provincial NDP:** the 1961 founding involved CCF, labour and New Party clubs. This is an organizational transition, not merely a graphic change.
- **B.C. Democratic Alliance → British Columbia Democratic Coalition → Democratic Reform B.C.:** the official registered-name changes are in September and December 2004. **Progressive Democratic Alliance** is a separate entity.
- **United Party of British Columbia (1980s)** is separate from **BC United (2023)**.
- Coalition, pre-1903 government/opposition alignments and generic independent/labour labels require their own historical attribution. A modern party symbol is not backdated to them.

Exact evidence and URLs for these statements are in the history table and PDF.

## Original files and previews

Original downloads are retained as supplied. Source extractions are precise crops or exports from cited material, with the original page and location recorded. Historical scans retain paper marks and limited resolution. SVG does not always mean pure vector: the 2009 Liberal file contains an embedded sun bitmap, and the 2013 SVG export can contain rasterized source shading. Cropped PDF companions preserve the native source representation where available. Official Conservative PDF logo objects recovered here are raster.

The NDP EPS originals include a grey background in their source rendering; their official transparent PNGs are preferable for general screen use. The Greens JPG files are CMYK with profiles; supplied previews apply their colour profiles. White logos have a dark background added in previews only. EPS sheets containing several variants are preserved intact. Source ownership and source-specific rights notices remain in the register; this package does not create a new blanket licence or imply party endorsement.

Full newspapers, platform books, web-page scrapes and unrelated research images are excluded. Only the attributed logo/name specimens and their display derivatives are packaged.

## Known gaps

Early Conservative and Liberal artwork, a standardized BC CCF logo master, NDP material from 1961–1995, early Greens chronology, some Social Credit variants (including the clasped-hands lead), Progressive Democratic Alliance and the 1980s United Party remain incomplete. The early Socialist, Social Democratic, Social Constructive and Labour-family records, People's Party, Non Partisan Independent and Unionist identities also retain explicit image gaps. These absences do not establish that the parties never used logos.

The collection is a sourced recovery package, not a certified complete archive of every version ever used. Its structured records are intended to make later additions straightforward.
