## Corrections to carry into the next data revision

### 1997 and 2001 are different snapshots

The 1997 structure is **11 boards + 34 councils + 7 societies = 52 bodies**. The later constituent inventory is **11 boards + 33 councils + 7 societies = 51 bodies**. These counts should not be reconciled by dropping an unknown body or inventing a merger. The designation chapter records the actual roster evolution, including Nisga'a. The societies also require their legal names at the selected date: Central Kootenay became Kootenay Boundary on 21 January 1998. [HT12; HD register]

### Composition rules are not always creation instruments

The earlier research entry **HL20** described OIC 1298/1994 as proof that named boards existed by 13 October 1994. That claim needs correction. The order amended the **composition regulation**, not the individual designation instruments. Some bodies listed there were designated afterward. Northern Interior's actual designation, for example, is M357/1994, effective 17 November 1994. Use the original designation as the start evidence, not an anticipatory composition list. The supplied correction record identifies this explicitly. [HD register]

### Corporate life may extend beyond an operational replacement

The seven regional community health services societies did not legally disappear merely because governance and operations were reorganized in December 2001. The Corporate Registry records their dissolution on **15 February 2002**. Likewise, PHSA's continuing institutional history includes multiple corporate identities and two documented 2018 amalgamations. Preserve both an institutional timeline and the registered legal entities that implement it. [HT09–HT16; RF14–RF16]

### “Created,” “renamed,” “acquired” and “controlled” are different assertions

PRHC is a particularly useful test case. Its continuing company was incorporated as Pemco in 1961; the Province acquired it in 1973; its present name began in December 1976; BC Housing gained control in January 2024; accounting consolidation followed in March. One creation date and one undated parent would conceal most of that history. The exact 1973 acquisition day and any later share-transfer instrument remain unproved. [CS12–CS19]

## Additional history recovered beyond the eight gaps

The Health department chapter extends the provincial/local structure back to the **1893 Health Act**, with an original statute for the Provincial Board of Health and local boards. It identifies a **Social Welfare Section in the Provincial Secretary's department by 1934**, the scoped **1946 transfers from Provincial Secretary**, a **1947 reversal of two transferred functions**, and the precise **20 March 1959** commencement of both the Health Services and Hospital Insurance and Social Welfare departments. These are branch and function movements that a simple succession of ministry names would miss. [RF01–RF12]

The PHSA chapter supplies the original society's **12 December 2001** registration, the exhaustive five-branch list in the **1 April 2018** amalgamation, and the **29 June 2018** amalgamation with BC Clinical and Support Services Society. It also recovers older corporate registration and rename dates for the cancer, mental-health, transplant, children's/women's and disease-control bodies. It does not substitute a corporate registration date for an older hospital opening or clinical-program origin. [RF14–RF24]

An additional original order, **OIC 3199/1976, approved 28 October 1976**, supplies **17 resulting ministry names** across government. The complete mapping includes Health, Finance, Education, Forests, Labour, Human Resources, Environment, and the Municipal Affairs/Housing combination. The later 1977 statutory title amendments and their proclamation-authorizing order are recorded separately. This is a useful shared source for expanding the history beyond the health portfolio. [RF25–RF28]

The housing research identifies an exact **21 July 1999** ministerial-responsibility change for BC Housing and PRHC, plus HPO's **2010 functional split** between BC Housing and Finance. The latter needs more than a single all-functions-successor relationship. [CS16, CS20]

## How to connect this history through the people of British Columbia

The research supports a graph with several types of connection. It does not support making every body an internal ministry branch. My recommendation is to keep the people of British Columbia at the centre of navigation, while preserving the actual kind of relationship along each route.

| Connection to show | Evidence needed / meaning | Example from this follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Democratic accountability | Representation and responsible-government relationships | The route through elected institutions should be distinguishable from service delivery. |
| Ministerial accountability | Reporting, budget, direction or appointment powers | Boards and councils under the Health Authorities Act; society governors under their bylaws. |
| Service responsibility | Mandate or funding/transfer agreement | Health service providers and the communities they serve. |
| Program policy | Responsibility for a specified program, with its date interval | Children and Families' transferred programs; Health Planning after June 2001. |
| Ownership | Shares or another express ownership instrument | Province's acquisition of Pemco/PRHC, separately from management. |
| Governance control | Appointment rights or other verified control | BC Housing's control of PRHC from 26 January 2024. |
| Corporate succession | An amalgamation, dissolution or vesting instrument | The two PHSA amalgamations in 2018. |
| Administrative succession | Transfer of a function, staff or operations | The 1959 Social Welfare split and HPO's 2010 divided functions. |

For the interface, a selected entity should be able to show its evidence-backed route to public representation and/or the people it serves, even when intermediate nodes are several steps away. A direct visual connection can be a useful navigation feature, but it should not silently assert direct public election, Crown ownership or ministerial control. Independent adjudication and other forms of accountability should remain distinguishable.

This is a recommendation for a later implementation pass. The current deliverable makes the underlying relationships reviewable before any graph or data change.

## Evidence policy used in this report

**Date roles are explicit.** Assent, approval/signature, statutory commencement, regulation deposit, incorporation, appointment, operational opening, publication and dissolution can occur on different days. The data records the role of each recovered date. A year-only source remains year precision.

**Identity is checked before succession is inferred.** Similar names, overlapping territory and matching end/start dates are useful leads, but they do not establish an exhaustive merger. Registry numbers and express amending/amalgamation instruments carry more weight.

**Scans control over OCR when they conflict.** The original handwritten or stamped dates were visually checked where they affected the result. Ambiguous handwriting remains ambiguous. The evidence files preserve both the original and the interpretation.

**Negative findings are bounded.** “Not located in this research” means that the listed public record routes did not yield the needed instrument. It does not mean the event never occurred. The open gaps below name the precise documents that would settle them.

## What remains genuinely open

1. **The individual execution days for the 1996 council-to-board amalgamations.** Needed: operative ministerial specifications, legal implementation correspondence, or contemporaneous minutes/closing records identifying the statutory merger day. A company certificate may be the wrong record for a statutory council.
2. **The seven societies' asset-transfer instruments and dates.** Registry dissolution and appointment changes establish different events. Needed: final filings, transfer agreements, consents or audited closing notes, using the recovered S-numbers.
3. **Some regulation deposit/effective dates.** The renaming text and order-made dates are recovered; where the original does not separately prove commencement, the field remains unfilled.
4. **Sea to Sky's original council effective day.** The original designation reference and 1997 continuation are recovered, but the exact early effective day remains unproved.
5. **The 1974 administrative sheriff amalgamation instrument/day.** The province-wide jurisdiction statute is identified, but it is not the same legal assertion.
6. **Pemco's exact 1973 acquisition date/instrument and the underlying 1961 incorporation certificate.** The contemporary Registrar notice already establishes incorporation date and identity; the certificate is a distinct missing document.
7. **Some PHSA predecessor transaction details.** These include the complete participants in the 1997 children's/women's and April 2001 BCCDC amalgamations, the mapping from numbered to named branch societies in December 2001, and associated asset conveyances.

These gaps do not prevent the supported changes above. They prevent the remaining unknowns from being replaced by plausible but unproved dates or relationships.

### 2. The ministry relationship: evidence for each legal form

#### Boards and councils: statutory accountability, not ministry departments

The Health Authorities Act defines “board” and “council” by legal designation. Its requirements apply to **every** body in those classes; it is not necessary to find 44 separate annual reports repeating the same statutory obligation. The member of the graph must first be identified as a legally designated board or council. The companion designation research supplies that identity work. [HT25]

The statute supplies concrete relation types:

| Relationship | Provision in the 2001 historical consolidation |
|---|---|
| Minister designates regional board; appoints its members | s.4 |
| Board reports on its activities and submits budgets to minister | s.5(1)(a)(v), (b) |
| Minister designates community health council; appoints its members | s.6 |
| Council reports on its activities and submits budgets to minister | s.7(1)(a)(ii), (d) |
| Board/council follows minister's general or special directions | s.7.1 |
| Minister approves board/council bylaws | s.8(2) |
| Minister receives accounts, can inspect financial records, sets report form | s.10(1), (2), (5) |

The corporations are legally distinct from ministry branches. Their capacity, own officers and employees, and the general nonapplication of the Public Service Act are expressly addressed in ss.8 and11. Therefore a graph edge should say, for example, **“reports to the responsible minister,” “minister appoints members,”** or **“funded under provincial agreement”**. It should not imply that a council was an internal Ministry of Health division. [HT25]

The machine-readable file contains individual accountability records for all **44 bodies in the December 2001 statutory schedule**: 11 regional boards and33 councils, including the two transitional health authorities that were legally council designations. Seven society records bring this inventory to51. This is a **2001 constituent inventory**; the 1997 11-board/34-council/7-society roster totals52. Those counts refer to different snapshots.

#### Do not apply the post-August 1997 hierarchy backward

The older statute required councils to report to **both the regional board and the minister**, and to submit budgets for regional services to the board. Regional boards likewise reported to councils as well as the minister, and allocated grants among councils. Councils also participated in regional board membership. These were real statutory relationships, not merely lines inferred from a regional map. [HT32]

**SBC1997c23**, ss.5–11, changed this model. Its sections6 and8 remove the interposed reporting requirements; council budgets go directly to the minister; appointments are revised; ministerial approval of bylaws and a general/special direction power are introduced. Assent was **28 July 1997**, but the operative change was **25 August 1997**, under **B.C. Reg.294/97**. The official table of legislative changes records that commencement precisely. [HT30–HT31]

The 1997 Auditor General review concerns administrative processes already operating from 1 April. It therefore complements the legislation rather than allowing us to assume every administrative and statutory change happened on one day. The accurate timeline can represent April funding/operational transfer and August legislative revision separately.

#### Societies: direct bylaws appointments and funding agreements

For the seven societies, it would be insufficient to cite board/council provisions as if every “health authority” were legally the same kind of corporation. All seven original appointment orders were recovered:

- M135, M136, M137, M138, M144, M145 andM146/1997 appoint members effective **1 April 1997**.
- Each names **Joy K. MacPhail, Minister of Health and Minister Responsible for Seniors**, as the decision-maker.
- Each invokes the society's **bylaws**. M145 andM146 are visually verified as signed **27 March 1997**. [HT17–HT23]

There is also an unusually direct collective source. The Auditor General explains that the **Funding and Transfer Agreement** is a contract between each individual health authority and the Province, represented by the Minister of Health. The paragraph then expressly distinguishes regional boards, councils and societies and describes the obligations of councils and societies under their agreements. See **printed p.31, PDFp.35**, visually verified. That is evidence of each society's provincial funding/accountability relationship, not just of its presence in a list. [HT24]

The same report, **printed p.34, PDFp.38**, says that Health leads overall funding and contracting, including program funds for the Ministry for Children and Families. Children and Families leads policy for the programs transferred to it; its regional operating officers review the relevant portions of allocation plans, while Health signs off the whole plan. The report records uncertainty in practice about the limits of accountability. A graph should therefore use a program-specific Children and Families policy connection where supported, not replace Health with an exclusive Children and Families parent. [HT24]

Printed p.33 names the program families: public health speech, audiology, nutrition and dental services; public health nursing for children and youth; related forensic psychiatric services; child and youth mental health; infant and child development; and alcohol and drug programs. It describes new relationships particularly with regional boards and community health services societies. The report does not give a program-by-program assignment to every one of the51 historical entities. The machine-readable data therefore preserves this as a **conditional program relationship**, not51 identical program portfolios. OIC565/2001, Appendix A s.41, transfers alcohol, drug and gambling addiction responsibilities back to Health Services on5June2001; that provision does not end every other Children and Families relationship. [HT24, pp.33–34; HT26, s.41]

The Auditor General also expressly identifies **local communities** as an accountability audience on printedp.1. That provides a source-backed public-accountability connection relevant to putting the people served at the centre of the graph. The boards and councils' statutory public-meeting rule in s.8(3) supplies a concrete participation mechanism. Neither source means that every governor was directly elected by the public. [HT24–HT25, HT32]

**OIC565/2001, approved 5 June 2001**, makes the legal distinction precise:

| Appendix A s.38 | Transfers planning, policy/program development, resource allocation/capital planning, evaluation and delivery-monitoring functions to Planning |

The two 2001/02 annual reports explain the complementary implementation roles. For statutory boards/councils the legal administrator boundary is directly proved on **5 June**. ### 3. The 1996 council mergers: what was established and what remains missing

The predecessor groups remain those in M432, M441 andM444/1996. The original orders make rescission conditional **on an amalgamation**. Some application language appears within those conditional clauses themselves. Neither a document's signing date nor a later schedule listing only the successor is independent proof that the legal merger happened on a particular day. [HT34–HT36]

Three different legal mechanisms need to be kept separate:

| Type of transaction | Historical mechanism | What would settle an exact date |
|---|---|---|
| Two or more old councils → a new council | Reg338/96 s.3: geographic containment and appointment of new council members | The first effective new-council appointment, read with the designation and scope of s.3 |
| Council(s) → a regional board | Reg338/96 s.4, added by Reg361/96: containment and minister specifies application; assets vest without further act/deed | An operative ministerial specification or contemporaneous implementation record establishing when the conditional order took effect |
| A designated corporation, such as a hospital society → board/council | HAA s.16: corporation and board/council consents, minister consent; special corporate resolution; certified evidence; Registrar notification | Consent/certificate and stated effective date, or the registrar's corresponding record |

The first two are not automatically governed by the third procedure. Asking only for corporate merger certificates could miss the relevant evidence for statutory councils. Conversely, identifying a Society Act corporation with the same words in its name is not enough to identify the statutory council. [HT25, s.16; HT29, ss.3–4]

There is valuable later confirmation: **SBC1997c23 s.18, printed/PDFp.12**, validates Reg338/96 effective **29 November 1996** and its Reg361/96 amendment effective **13 December 1996**. It also validates the amalgamations referred to by the regulation. It does **not** enumerate the individual councils' completion dates. This closes a question about legislative validation of the mechanism, not the user's requested day-by-day execution list. [HT30]

The Auditor General's timeline on printedp.28 includes a planned March1997 amalgamation milestone. It is explicitly a ministry implementation schedule. Treating that planned day as a completed transaction for all councils would overstate the evidence. The same report's confirmed April/October operational devolution dates describe service responsibility, not every legal council merger. [HT24]

#### 1974: separate the organizational event from the jurisdictional statute

The government's sheriff history dates amalgamation of the nine county sheriff offices into the provincial service to **1974**. [CS03]

A precise legal reform is now identified: **Statute Law Amendment Act, 1974, SBC 1974 c. 87, s. 35**, printed p. 547. It amended s. 3 of the Sheriffs Act to give each sheriff jurisdiction throughout the Province. Section 42(1), printed p. 550, brought s. 35 into force on Royal Assent, **20 June 1974**, recorded on the Act's first page. [CS04]

That statute does **not** expressly abolish or combine nine administrative offices. Recommended events are therefore:

- `organizational_amalgamation`, date `1974`, precision `year`, supported by official institutional history;
- `jurisdiction_expanded`, date `1974-06-20`, precision `day`, instrument `SBC 1974 c87 s35`.

Contemporary Hansard is useful corroboration, not a substitute for an unlocated administrative instrument. On 6 June 1974 the Attorney General described the new service, its first trained class and expansion plans. On 20 June the debate explained cross-county jurisdiction and court-security/prisoner-transport duties. [CS05, CS06]

**Still open:** a cabinet order, Treasury Board decision, departmental reorganization memorandum or contemporary annual report that explicitly records the administrative combination and its effective day. The scanned statute supplies a legal milestone; it does not close that separate gap.

### 2. Modern B.C. Ferry Authority: exact creation and distinct relationships

The **Coastal Ferry Act, SBC 2003 c. 14**, received Royal Assent on **27 March 2003**. Section 2(1) establishes the B.C. Ferry Authority; s. 89 brings the Act into force on **1 April 2003**. The Authority has no share capital under s. 2(4). Its statutory role and ownership of the voting share connect it to the operator. [CS07]

The regulator's history dates conversion of **British Columbia Ferry Corporation** into **British Columbia Ferry Services Inc.** to **2 April 2003**. This is a different entity/event from the Authority's establishment on 1 April. Section 20(3) of the Act ties the end of the operator's Crown-agency status to conversion. [CS07, CS08]

Recommended representation:

| Entity/event | Date | Event type |
|---|---|---|
| Coastal Ferry Act assented to | 2003-03-27 | legislation_assented |
| Modern B.C. Ferry Authority established | 2003-04-01 | statutory_establishment |
| Ferry operator converted to British Columbia Ferry Services Inc. | 2003-04-02 | corporate_conversion |
| Operator's Crown-agency status ceased | On conversion | legal_status_change |

Keep the **modern 2003 Authority** distinct from the similarly named **1964–1977 ferry operator authority**. Likewise, separate voting-share ownership/oversight, government contracts/subsidies and independent regulation rather than using one generic parent link. Initial 2003 board mechanics should be sourced from the contemporary Act, not assumed from the current amended consolidation. [CS07, CS08]
