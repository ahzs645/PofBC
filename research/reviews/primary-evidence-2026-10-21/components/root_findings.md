# Additional primary evidence: Health department commencement and PHSA corporate history

Research checked 21 October 2026 (UTC). This supplement records evidence, not changes made to the PofBC repository. Source identifiers refer to `root_sources.json` in this supplement.

## 1. The 1946 commencement gap is closed

The Department of Health and Welfare Act, SBC 1946 c31, received assent on **11 April 1946**. Its separate commencement is now established by both the original authorizing order and the actual published proclamation:

| Event | Exact date | Primary evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Assent | 11 April 1946 | Act, heading; section 12 requires proclamation. RF06 |
| Order directing proclamation | 10 September 1946 | OIC 2060/1946. RF01 |
| Proclamation issued | 10 September 1946 | Proclamation witnessed on that day, Gazette notice 3543. RF02 |
| Act operative | **1 October 1946** | Both OIC 2060 and the proclamation expressly state this date. RF01–RF02 |
| Gazette publication | 3 October 1946 | British Columbia Gazette, vol. LXXXVI, no. 40, printed p. 2905; PDF page 5. RF02 |

The Gazette scan was read visually, including the continuation of the proclamation in the top right column. The adjacent proclamation, notice 3544, commences the **Health Act Amendment Act, 1946** on the same 1 October date, but was itself issued on 28 September. Those are two different proclamations. The handwritten annotation “Proclamation #14” on OIC 2060 provides an additional archival locator; the printed Gazette notice number is 3543. RF01–RF02.

**Permitted change:** retain assent as a legislative event and use 1946-10-01 for the new department's statutory commencement. Record the issuance and publication dates separately. This finding replaces the earlier month-only October 1946 evidence.

### The associated functional transfer is also documented

OIC **2214/1946**, approved **28 September 1946**, expressly recites the October commencement and assigns duties from the Provincial Secretary to Health and Welfare under section 8. Its first list assigns administration of the named statutes; its second list assigns only duties concerning public health, social welfare and social assistance. A whole-ministry or whole-statute transfer must not be inferred for the second list. Examples there include the Hospital Act and Public Schools Act. The order is also printed in the same Gazette issue at p. 2907. RF03.

OIC **2218/1946** promotes the Provincial Health Officer to Deputy Minister of Health effective **1 October 1946**, and separately appoints him Provincial Health Officer under the amended Health Act. It supplies an additional dated link between the new department and that office. RF04.

OIC **752/1947**, approved **23 April 1947**, removes the Physiotherapists and Massage Practitioners Act and Welfare Institutions Licensing Act from the second list in OIC 2214 and reassigns those duties to the Provincial Secretary. This is a documented reversal of a limited functional transfer; the 1946 relationship must not simply be extended indefinitely. RF05.

## 2. The 1959 split can now be dated from both commencement orders

**20 March 1959** is established independently for both resulting departments:

| Instrument | Result |
|---|---|
| OIC 559/1959, approved 20 March | Directs proclamation of the Department of Health Services and Hospital Insurance Act for that same day. RF07 |
| OIC 558/1959, approved 20 March | Directs proclamation of the Department of Social Welfare Act for that same day. RF08 |
| Health statute, SBC 1959 c38, ss. 6–11 | Names Health Branch, Branch of Mental Health Services and British Columbia Hospital Insurance Service; redirects mental-health responsibilities formerly held by Provincial Secretary. RF09 |
| Social Welfare statute, SBC 1959 c76, ss. 7–8 | Redirects the former Health and Welfare minister's and deputy's responsibilities for the Social Welfare Branch to the new department's officials. RF10 |

**Permitted change:** represent an outgoing social-welfare branch and an incoming mental-health function, as well as the health department's changed name. A simple rename of the whole 1946 department loses the separately documented Social Welfare successor.

## 3. Earlier evidence now identified

The original **Health Act, 1893**, SBC 1893 c15, dated **18 April 1893**, explicitly provides for the Provincial Board of Health in section 3. Section 4 makes its chair the Chief Health Officer. Section 37 distinguishes municipal councils acting as local boards from appointed boards in other health districts; the following provisions permit unions of municipalities. This supplies an original statutory source for the earlier provincial/local public-health structure. It does not prove uninterrupted corporate descent of any particular modern regional authority. RF11.

OIC **1294/1934**, approved **22 October 1934**, appoints a secretary to the **Social Welfare Section, Department of Provincial Secretary**, effective 12 November. It directly proves that named section and parent relationship by late 1934. It does **not** establish the creation date of a combined “Health and Welfare Branch”; that broader formulation remains unverified. RF12.

The original **1973 c109** amendment, assented to **7 November 1973**, replaces Health Services and Hospital Insurance with Health in the department's statute. It is a one-page amendment with no separate commencement clause. This establishes the original renaming text and assent date. The separate 1976 executive name directive and 1977 statutory amendments are now documented below. RF13.


## 3A. A wider finding: the 1976 department-to-ministry name order

**OIC 3199/1976**, approved **28 October 1976**, expressly directs the following names. The original approval stamp was visually checked; it says 28 October, not 29 October. This supplies a reusable original source for many parts of PofBC beyond Health. RF25.

| Previous department name(s) | Resulting ministry name |
|---|---|
| Department of the Provincial Secretary | Ministry of the Provincial Secretary and Travel Industry |
| Department of Finance | Ministry of Finance |
| Department of the Attorney-General | Ministry of the Attorney-General |
| Department of Agriculture | Ministry of Agriculture |
| Department of Education | Ministry of Education |
| Department of Municipal Affairs + Department of Housing | Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing |
| Department of Mines and Petroleum Resources | Ministry of Mines and Petroleum Resources |
| Department of Forests | Ministry of Forests |
| Department of Labour | Ministry of Labour |
| Department of Health | Ministry of Health |
| Department of Highways and Public Works | Ministry of Highways and Public Works |
| Department of Economic Development | Ministry of Economic Development |
| Department of Transport and Communications | Ministry of Energy, Transport and Communications |
| Department of Human Resources | Ministry of Human Resources |
| Department of Environment | Ministry of the Environment |
| Department of Consumer Services | Ministry of Consumer and Corporate Affairs |
| Department of Recreation and Travel Industry | Ministry of Recreation and Conservation |

The Municipal Affairs/Housing line combines two former departmental names in the resulting name. The order itself is a naming directive; it should not be expanded into an unsupported statement that every asset or function was transferred by that text alone.

The later **Ministerial Titles Amendment Act, 1977**, assented to **27 September 1977**, implements statutory text and title changes. Section 10 and the Tenth Schedule rename the Department of Health Act as the Ministry of Health Act; section 1 and the First Schedule replace departmental references; section 45 updates a Health Act reference. Section 91 requires proclamation. RF26.

The original **OIC 3240/1977**, approved **21 October 1977** following an Executive Council meeting on 20 October, directs proclamation with specifically listed exceptions. The Health changes are not excluded. The approval stamp alone is not a separately stated commencement date: the order does not name an effective day in its operative paragraph. A statutory consolidation attached to **OIC 2721/1979** records sections 1 and 28 of the 1977 Act as proclaimed effective **21 October 1977**. That is useful official corroboration for those provisions; the actual 1977 Gazette proclamation remains a distinct document not retrieved here. RF27–RF28.

The defensible sequence is therefore the **1973 departmental-name statutory amendment**, the **28 October 1976 executive ministry-name directive**, and the **1977 statutory title/conforming amendments with a proclamation authorization**. These should not be collapsed into one undifferentiated founding event.

## 4. PHSA has a legal identity sequence, as well as an institutional history

The public BC Corporate Registry records exposed through OrgBook distinguish three registered PHSA societies. The exact amalgamation notices corroborate both 2018 transitions:

| Society number | Registry / legal event | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **S0044011** | Original PHSA registration **12 December 2001**; becomes historical on 1 April 2018 through an amalgamation filing. | RF14 |
| **S0069120** | PHSA resulting from the amalgamation of the original PHSA and five named branch societies, **1 April 2018, 2:23 p.m. Pacific**. | RF15 |
| **S0069572** | PHSA resulting from amalgamation of S0069120 and BC Clinical and Support Services Society, **29 June 2018, 9:49 a.m. Pacific**. | RF16 |

The April Gazette notice names exactly these five branch societies: British Columbia Cancer Agency Branch (S0044073); British Columbia Centre for Disease Control and Prevention Society Branch (S0044074); British Columbia Mental Health Society Branch (S0044076); British Columbia Transplant Society Branch (S0044077); and Children's & Women's Health Centre of British Columbia Branch (S0044078). RF15.

The June notice identifies **BC Clinical and Support Services Society as S0054278**. RF16.

**Permitted change:** retain an institutional PHSA timeline beginning in 2001, while giving corporate identity records their own registry numbers and amalgamation events. The current entity's 2018 registration must not erase the 2001 institutional origin. Conversely, treating every registry number as the same unchanged corporation obscures actual legal transactions.

### Registry evidence rules

BC Corporate Registry is the issuer shown on the retrieved records. OrgBook's own English terminology identifies `Event:CONVSCREGI` as Conversion Society Registration, `Event:CONVSDIS` as Conversion Society Dissolution, and `Event:CONVSCNC` as Conversion Society Name Change. These are legacy events imported into the newer system; the 2020 credential issuance timestamps are not the historical event dates. The companion health-transactions research records the official label source. Preserve the raw event code, registry number, historical effective date and retrieval date alongside any normalized event. A society dissolution event alone does not identify an asset recipient or the mechanism of an earlier operational transfer.
