# Pre-2001 health authorities: designation and renaming follow-up

Research completed 21 October 2026. Scope: the 11-board, 34-council and 7-society system, its 1994–95 roots, and express name amendments. The companion `health_transactions` research handles accountability, execution and societies. No repository data was changed.

## What can now change

The continuing councils can be shown before 2000 with direct legal sources. Seven 1997 orders reconstruct all 34 councils; 30 are expressly **continued**, while four are newly designated combined councils. For 29 of the 30 continued councils, the original 1994–95 order provides an exact operative effective date, visually checked against the scan. The remaining original Sea to Sky regulation is identified, but its online scan has a conflicting cover page.

All nine required board-to-“Health Region” name chains now have express original amendment orders. Two chains have intermediate names: Simon Fraser/Burnaby → Simon Fraser Health Board → Simon Fraser Health Region; North Okanagan → North Okanagan/Columbia Shuswap → North Okanagan Health Region. These are name histories, not justification for unrelated new corporations.

**Date discipline:** Dates labelled “made” come from the signed order. They are not silently equated with deposit or legal commencement. The 1994–95 original designation dates are expressly stated in the operative text. The 1997 continuation dates are signed-order dates unless separately stated. Exact execution of a conditional amalgamation is a further event.

## Correct the earlier Northern Interior inference

The prior HL20 statement that Northern Interior existed by 13 October 1994 should be revised. [OIC1298/1994](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/oic/arc_oic/1298_1994) amends **composition** under Health Authorities Act section 4(2). It names appointing councils and board seats; it does not designate the board under section 4(1). [M357/1994](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/mo/hmo/m0357_1994) separately declares that the Northern Interior Regional Health Board is designated and expressly makes the regulation effective **17 November 1994**. Similar composition-before-designation patterns occur for other boards and councils.

Store two events: composition specified 13 October; statutory designation effective 17 November. This prevents composition schedules and planned governance diagrams from being treated as incorporation dates. The original Prince George Health Council designation on 24 March 1995 is compatible with its anticipated appearance in the October 1994 composition schedule.

## How 11+34+7 becomes the later 51-body set

The seven 1997 society names are Cariboo; Coast Garibaldi; North West; Upper Island/Central Coast; **Central Kootenay**; East Kootenay; and Peace Liard, each followed by “Community Health Services Society”. Central Kootenay is the original name of the later Kootenay Boundary society. The companion transaction register verifies all seven incorporation dates as 13 March 1997, and first ministerial board appointments effective 1 April 1997 in M135–138 and M144–146. The machine register includes the exact links.

The 34th council is Nisga’a. [M366/1999](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/mo/hmo/m0366_1999) repeals section 35 of Reg. 27/97 and removes its territorial description. The 1999 bulletin records Reg. 333/99 deposited 18 October 1999. Thus the 1997 set is 11+34+7=52, while the 2001 statutory list has 11+33=44 and the seven societies sit outside that statutory amalgamation list. This explains the counts; it does not mean Nisga’a healthcare stopped in 1999 or the council merged into Northern.
