# Panco and Dunhill identity verification

Prepared 22 November 2026. This research answers questions 3 and 5 and supplies connected events. It does not change the repository. Original source PDFs are linked in the source register; `identity_findings.json` contains dated claims, legal identity chains, source URLs, locators, and remaining gaps.

## Findings that change integration

**HCBC Construction is Fisher Developments under a later name.** Gazette company no.71787 links Fisher Developments Ltd. to HCBC Construction Ltd., effective 26 November 1976. It therefore belongs on the existing Fisher record. The held HCBC candidate should not become an extra subsidiary. The same Gazette page links no.91003, W.K.P. Construction Ltd., to Dunhill Development Corporation on that date. The child's later name has **no “Ltd.”** [S02, p1664]

**The original Dunhill is a different company.** No.73385 changed from Dunhill Development Corporation Ltd. to Housing Corporation of British Columbia on 18 November 1976. Gazette incorporation dates are 17 March 1967 for no.73385, 19 December 1969 for no.91003, and 17 November 1966 for no.71787. These are legal birth dates, not dates when the Province acquired them. [S01, p1629; S02, p1664]

| Registry number | Earlier name | Later name | Effective rename |
|---|---|---|---|
| 73385 | Dunhill Development Corporation Ltd. | Housing Corporation of British Columbia | 18 Nov 1976 |
| 91003 | W.K.P. Construction Ltd. | Dunhill Development Corporation | 26 Nov 1976 |
| 71787 | Fisher Developments Ltd. | HCBC Construction Ltd. | 26 Nov 1976 |

## W.K.P. ownership after 1974 is documented

The 1975/76 Public Accounts contain Dunhill's consolidated statement for **31 October 1975**, printed F103. They explicitly list W.K.P. Construction and Fisher Developments among seven **100%-owned** subsidiaries. This fills the earliest post-1974 gap. Subsequent accounts establish the same parent relationship under the renamed identities. [S03–S07]

| Statement date | Printed page | Wholly owned subsidiaries relevant here |
|---|---|---|
| 31 Oct 1975 | 1975/76 F103 | W.K.P.; Fisher; Crown Lumber; Dunhill Homes; Dunhill Holdings; Venture Realty; Georgia Park Holdings |
| 31 Oct 1976 | 1976/77 F133 | Renamed Dunhill; renamed HCBC; Crown Lumber; Venture Realty |
| 31 Oct 1977 | 1977/78 F152 | Dunhill; HCBC; Venture Realty |
| 31 Oct 1978 | 1978/79 F154 | Dunhill; HCBC; Venture Realty |
| 31 Oct 1984 | 1984/85 G247 | Dunhill; HCBC |

The parallel Statistics Canada verification adds Housing → Dunhill and Housing → HCBC snapshots at 31 December 1986 and 31 December 1987. The later snapshot is on **printed p103**, not p102. Those later-dated appendices occur inside the 1984 and 1985 financial publications. They are observations, not incorporation dates. See the separate Statistics Canada report. [S12]

The graph should no longer imply that the parent is wholly unknown for the whole 1975–1989 period. Preserve the observed dates and evidence. These observations do not by themselves prove every intervening day or rule out a later transfer. The 12 June 1989 dissolution of no.91003 is recorded separately in the 22 June 1989 Gazette. [S09, p819]

## Related Dunhill subsidiary events

The 1976 statement says Dunhill Homes Ltd., Dunhill Holdings Ltd., and Georgia Park Holdings Ltd. were included in the 1975 comparatives but **wound up in 1976**. Record the year only. The 1977 statement dates the sale of Crown Lumber Limited at book value to **1 November 1976**. [S04, F133; S05, F152]

Housing's 1978 notes date its ministerial wind-down directive to **29 September 1978**. They also date the sale of **Venture Realty Ltd.** and certain assets of the professional-services division to **30 November 1978**. The notes use “Venture” in the singular; retain earlier spellings as source variants without inventing a legal rename. Winding down operations is distinct from legal dissolution. [S06, F154]

## Panco acquisition and sale: exact dates and amounts

The 1978/79 Public Accounts, F215, give substantially more detail than the investment schedules. The Province purchased all issued Panco Poultry Ltd. shares on **17 February 1975**. The company, by then described as **Surrey Farm Products Investments Ltd. (formerly Panco Poultry Ltd.)**, sold its assets and business on **29 January 1979**. [S06, F215 notes 1–3]

| Transaction component | CAD |
|---|---:|
| Gross proceeds | 12,076,253 |
| Disbursements and adjustments | 351,593 |
| Net proceeds | 11,724,660 |
| Net book cost | 2,714,030 |
| Gain | 9,010,630 |
| Separate dividend paid 4 May 1979 | 14,800,000 |

**The $14.8 million is a dividend transferred to the Minister of Finance on 4 May 1979, not the gross asset price in the sale note.** The 1979 Auditor General appendix corroborates the asset disposal and subsequent dividend. [S06, F215 note 4; S11, A9 note 7(D)]

The 1979/80 receipt schedule's shorter “Surrey Farm Products Ltd.” wording accompanies this $14.8 million receipt. The same accounts' investment schedule retains the full Surrey Farm Products Investments name and identifies the former Panco shareholding. Taken together with the detailed 1979 note, this is not sufficient evidence of a separate company or purchaser. Treat it as a source-as-printed label reconciled to the existing company, and flag the abbreviation. No inspected note names the asset purchaser. This finding does not claim that no unrelated company with the shorter name ever existed. [S10, A6, B4, B8, C3; S06, F215]

## Renames are established; two effective dates remain open

The audited statements establish the chain **Panco Poultry Ltd. → Surrey Farm Products Investments Ltd. → British Columbia Food Exhibitions Ltd.** Their explicit “formerly” clauses are strong identity evidence. They do **not** supply the two legal effective rename dates. The Surrey statements are for 31 March 1979 with an audit report dated 23 October 1979. Food Exhibitions statements are for 31 March 1985, with the auditor's report dated 17 June 1985. Use those as source observations, not invented effective rename dates. A historical Registry profile remains the next narrow retrieval if exact name-validity intervals are required. No paid Registry search was performed. [S06, F209/F215; S07, G52/G55]

Food Exhibitions' legal dissolution can now be dated. The Gazette of 9 July 1992, p840, lists **British Columbia Food Exhibitions Ltd., no.63265**, under dissolutions effective **19 June 1992**. The full page is necessary because the search snippet misleadingly groups the name beside a continuations heading. The Gazette number is verified at dissolution; the audited identity chain links the earlier names. Legal dissolution does not establish uninterrupted Crown ownership through 1992. [S08]

## Panco subsidiaries: later identity and windups

The 1979 consolidation note identifies **Surrey Farm Products Hatchery Ltd. (formerly Pacific Hatchery Ltd.)** and Western Feed Co. Ltd. as wholly owned subsidiaries. This is an alias chain for the existing hatchery company, not a reason to add another node. The earlier package's “Pacific Hatching” spelling still needs explicit reconciliation. [S06, F215 note 1]

The 1985 Food Exhibitions note says Surrey Farm Products Hatchery Ltd. and Western Feed Co. Ltd. were **wound up during the year ended 31 March 1985**. Store the period 1 April 1984–31 March 1985, with fiscal-year precision, and do not convert this accounting statement into an exact registry dissolution date. Later inactive Statistics Canada roster entries may preserve stale names and should remain as-printed observations. [S07, G55 note 1]

The inspected later lists do not resolve B.C. Testing Farms' exit or United Provincial Investments' omission. Absence from a subsequent consolidation list is not itself a dated sale or dissolution.
