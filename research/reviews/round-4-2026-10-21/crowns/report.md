## 1. PRHC and the housing-company acquisitions

### Pemco / PRHC: better checkpoints, with the original purchase still missing

The prior package already establishes the registry chain: Pemco Holdings Ltd., company 52129, incorporated **13 December 1961**; the name British Columbia Housing Corporation effective **7 May 1976**; and Provincial Rental Housing Corporation effective **21 December 1976**. Those dates are carried forward, not claimed as new discoveries. They should continue to control over later historical summaries that call the 1973 provincial acquisition an “incorporation.” See the recovered round-three Crown report and verified registry ledger.

The new original financial evidence adds two useful distinctions. Public Accounts 1975/76, printed **B10**, lists all **three issued Pemco common shares**, valued at **$633,511**, in the Housing Fund investments at both **31 March 1975** and **31 March 1976**. This is direct evidence of the provincial shareholding at those two dates. It does not identify the 1973 closing day, the purchase authorization, the executed purchase agreement, or an uninterrupted ministerial-accountability interval. A fund’s accounting placement alone should not be converted into every type of parent relationship. [R4E-002]

Printed **F154**, note 5, states that the name change followed a **special resolution of 12 April 1976** and a Registrar certificate of **7 May 1976**. The resolution date is new; the certificate date corroborates the previously proved registry event. The atlas can retain both as different events if it has room for corporate procedural detail. It should not replace the effective rename with the earlier resolution. [R4E-002]

The search covered Pemco/PRHC name variants, acquisition terms, earlier Public Accounts OCR and original later financial statements. The early OCR results did not identify the missing transaction. That is not evidence that the transaction was absent from all original records: a company could be described under a vendor, fund, property, appropriation or differently indexed account. **The exact 1973 purchase day and the original 1961 certificate therefore remain unresolved.** The next decisive material would be the company file, share register/transfer, executed purchase agreement, Cabinet authorization or Housing Department acquisition schedule. This continuation supplies no false day-level substitute.

### Dunhill: three dates that should not be collapsed

The original 1974/75 Public Accounts resolve a substantially better sequence than the previous authorization-only evidence. Printed **F55**, note 1, says that an agreement dated **25 January 1974** led the Province, through **Woodbridge Development Corporation**, to acquire enough shares **by 22 May 1974** for Dunhill to qualify for Crown income-tax exemption. Woodbridge was subsequently wound up, and on **7 June 1974** Dunhill became wholly owned **directly** by the Province. [R4E-001]

The next year’s Public Accounts, **F104**, note 5, describes **22 May 1974** as the day the company became a provincial Crown corporation. Read together, the notes distinguish the Crown-status milestone from the later direct ownership of every share. The January agreement is not an incorporation date; May 22 is not the proved start of direct 100% provincial ownership; June 7 is not a separately proved legal dissolution date for Woodbridge. [R4E-001], [R4E-002]

| Event | Date supported | Evidence and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition agreement | 25 January 1974 | Contemporary audited financial-note reference; executed agreement not recovered. |
| Sufficient shares acquired through Woodbridge for Crown tax exemption | By 22 May 1974 | 1974 financial note uses a “by” statement; the subsequent note calls May 22 the Crown-corporation date. |
| Dunhill directly wholly owned by Province | 7 June 1974 | Explicit in 1974/75 Public Accounts F55. |
| Woodbridge winding up | Before the resulting direct-ownership arrangement, exact legal date unresolved | The note states it was subsequently wound up but does not supply a separate winding-up instrument or date. |

This also sharpens the identity problem. The original Dunhill parent’s consolidated statements list **W.K.P. Construction Ltd.** as one of eight wholly owned subsidiaries, alongside Crown Lumber Limited, Fisher Developments Ltd., Dunhill Homes Ltd., Dunhill Holdings Ltd., United Provincial Investments Ltd., Ventures Realty Ltd. and Georgia Park Holdings Ltd. The list is a financial-statement observation for the year ended **31 October 1974**, not the first date of ownership of every subsidiary. [R4E-001]

The previously found registry company that began as W.K.P. Construction and took the Dunhill name in 1976 must therefore remain distinct from the original Dunhill parent unless a matching amalgamation, dissolution, transfer or other identity bridge is recovered. The new subsidiary list explains why merely matching “Dunhill Development Corporation” can produce the wrong history. It does not itself supply the missing 1976 bridge. The prior OIC 178/1974 remains an authorization source; these new financial statements establish reported acquisition milestones beyond that authorization.

## 2. Transit: original establishment, name, responsibility and transfer orders

### Establishment of the Urban Transit Authority

The **Urban Transit Authority Act**, SBC 1978, c.39, received assent on **29 June 1978**. Section 2 establishes the corporation, and section 25 makes commencement dependent on proclamation. **OIC 1931/1978**, approved **13 July**, orders sections **1–5, 8–20, 22–24 and 25(1)** into force on **20 July 1978**. The establishing section is among them. This supplies an exact start tied to the operative instrument. It does not justify saying that every section of the Act commenced that day. [R4E-003], [R4E-005]

The immediately preceding **OIC 1930/1978** expressly charges the **Minister of Municipal Affairs and Housing** with administration of the Act. Its approval date is **13 July 1978**, a week before statutory establishment. This is express assignment evidence, not an inference from who signed the proclamation. The body’s starting date should still be July 20. [R4E-004]

For the appointment relationship, the original section 4 requires at least eight directors appointed by the **Lieutenant Governor in Council**, selected from people holding elected municipal office. That is an appointment line with a stated eligibility rule. It does not make the corporation an elected provincial institution merely because its directors also hold municipal office. [R4E-003], [R4E-005]

### Metro Transit Operating Company

The **Metro Transit Operating Company Act**, SBC 1979, c.18, received assent on **31 July 1979**. **OIC 29/1980**, approved **10 January 1980**, brought the Act into force on **18 January 1980**. Section 2 establishes MTOC, and section 4 gives the Lieutenant Governor in Council appointment of at least seven directors. [R4E-006], [R4E-007]

The Act separately provides machinery for transferring Hydro employees and property. Its commencement should not be used as the date on which all transit operations changed hands. The commonly described April 1980 operational transition still requires the actual transfer orders/agreements if the atlas is to attach a precise day to each part of it. The later 1991 official consolidation says the MTOC Act was administered by Human Resources, but that is an observation in that edition, not proof of its original 1980 assignment. [R4E-006], [R4E-019]

### BC Transit name and Crown-agent clause

The **Urban Transit Authority Amendment Act**, SBC 1982, c.53, received assent on **23 July 1982**. Sections 1 and 3 change the Act’s title and the authority’s corporate name to **British Columbia Transit**; the replacement establishing provision expressly makes it a Crown agent. **OIC 1534/1982** and the official consolidation’s historical note establish **12 August 1982** as the operative date. The correct event is continuation under a new name and amended status, not a fresh incorporation. [R4E-008], [R4E-009], [R4E-018]

The original assignment orders now provide a compact historical sequence:

| Instrument | Assignment | Date and scope |
|---|---|---|
| OIC 1930/1978 | Municipal Affairs and Housing administers UTA Act | Approved 13 July 1978; body establishes July 20. [R4E-004] |
| OIC 1535/1982 | Administration transferred from Municipal Affairs to Education | 12 August 1982; also transfers unspent Vote 66 funds for Urban Transit Authority and Custom Bus Services. [R4E-010] |
| OIC 850/1983 | Human Resources charged with BC Transit Act | 26 May 1983; repeals the prior B.C. Reg. 366/82 assignment. [R4E-011] |

The intermediate change in the ministry’s name from Municipal Affairs and Housing to Municipal Affairs is not independently dated here. The exact Education interval can be supported from the two successive orders. Later Human Resources responsibility should remain open-ended in this package until joined to another express transfer or reliable historical source.

### The 1985 consolidation does not prove MTOC’s legal extinction

The **British Columbia Transit Amendment Act (No.2)**, SBC 1985, c.35, assented **24 May 1985**, inserts section 2.1 into the Transit Act. It transfers MTOC’s property and rights to BC Transit, makes Transit liable for MTOC’s debts and obligations, and preserves creditors’ rights. **OIC 1093/1985**, approved **30 May**, brings the amendment into force on **1 June 1985**. That is a complete primary chain for the asset/right/obligation transfer. [R4E-012], [R4E-013]

The employee transfer is supported by the new section 6.1, **OIC 1094/1985**, and the Public Accounts’ express description of the merger as effective **1 June 1985**. The employee order itself says its “effective date” without printing June 1 in the operative text. Its May 30 approval stamp should not be silently substituted for the transfer date. The Public Accounts supports June 1 and the enabling provision commenced that day. [R4E-012], [R4E-013], [R4E-014], [R4E-017]

Crucially, **OIC 1096/1985 appoints a new MTOC board effective 1 June 1985**. It names nine directors and Stuart Milton Hodgson as chair. OIC 1095 appoints the parallel Transit board. This is positive evidence that the legislation’s transfer should not automatically be translated into MTOC’s legal dissolution. The later official consolidation and historical legislation table also retain the MTOC Act, although those sources do not prove continued operating activity. The table’s coverage ends in **2013**, regardless of the current website crawl date. [R4E-015], [R4E-016], [R4E-019], [R4E-046]

The model should therefore distinguish **MTOC operational assets transferred** from **MTOC legal entity ended**. The first is proved on June 1, 1985; the second remains unresolved. A historical narrative saying “merged” is compatible with the transfer of everything operational while a statutory shell persists. The atlas needs a separate legal-end field or a qualified event to avoid hiding that distinction.

## 3. Liquor: legislation in 1975, operative branches in 1976, budgets in 1977

Both the **Liquor Distribution Act**, SBC 1975, c.39, and the **Liquor Control and Licensing Act**, SBC 1975, c.38, received assent on **26 June 1975**. Their section 2 provisions establish the respective branches, but their commencement clauses require later action. The exact statutory start is supplied by the following orders, all approved **2 December 1976**. [R4E-020], [R4E-021]

| Order | Operative result |
|---|---|
| **3490/1976** | Liquor Control and Licensing Act commenced **31 December 1976**, except ss.31,35,38(1),61,63(2)(b),(c); section 2 is included. [R4E-022] |
| **3491/1976** | Minister of Consumer and Corporate Affairs expressly charged with administering the Licensing Act. [R4E-023] |
| **3493/1976** | Government Liquor Act and Liquor-control Plebiscites Act repealed **31 December 1976**. [R4E-024] |
| **3494/1976** | Liquor Distribution Act commenced **31 December 1976**, except ss.16(3),17(1); existing government liquor stores continued under the new statute. [R4E-025] |
| **3495/1976** | Minister of Consumer and Corporate Affairs expressly charged with administering the Distribution Act. [R4E-026] |

The 1991 official Distribution Act consolidation corroborates the commencement date. These orders also provide historical responsibility evidence for the two branches without inferring a parent from their present-day ministry. The assignment-order date precedes the effective branch-establishment date, so the same date should not simply be copied into every graph field. [R4E-027]

In the Legislative Assembly on **1 September 1977**, Minister K.R. Mair’s response to question 97 states that the two branches’ **budgets were not split until 1 April 1977**. That explains another possible date in administrative histories. A three-date record is appropriate: 1975 assent, December 1976 statutory commencement, April 1977 budget separation. None alone proves the exact earlier administrative renaming from Liquor Administration Branch to Liquor Distribution Branch. That narrower E9 question remains open. [R4E-028]

Nor should the two branches automatically be represented as share corporations. The sources establish branches, their statutory administration, their continuation of functions and their budget separation. A repeal of the predecessor Act is not the same wording as a separately retrieved corporate dissolution of the old Liquor Control Board. Those relationship and event types should remain explicit.

## 4. BC Hydro: the legal history requires the 1963–65 repair

The primary documents explain why a single choice between “1961” and “1962” cannot capture the complete history. They concern acquisition of an existing company, an original statutory amalgamation, judicial invalidity, a settlement, a newly established statutory authority and a later effective amalgamation. The atlas can preserve a readable Hydro lineage while giving those legal events their proper dates.

### 1961 takeover and 1962 original scheme

The **Power Development Act 1961**, second session c.4, assented **3 August 1961**, purports to vest every BC Electric share, issued or unissued and common or otherwise, in the provincial Crown. Its section 26 deems effect back to **2 p.m. Pacific Standard Time on 1 August 1961**, explicitly excluding the daylight-saving convention. That precision comes directly from the statute; it is a deemed operative date rather than the assent date. [R4E-029]

The **BC Hydro and Power Authority Act 1962**, c.8, assented **29 March 1962**, provides for BC Electric and the BC Power Commission to amalgamate as Hydro. **OIC 839/1962** brings that Act into force **30 March 1962**; OIC 840 appoints initial directors and the two co-chairmen, Gordon M. Shrum and Hugh L. Keenleyside. These are original instruments for the familiar 1962 historical start. [R4E-030], [R4E-031], [R4E-032]

### What the contemporary annual report says about the court and settlement

Hydro’s second annual report, for the year ended **31 March 1964**, states that on **29 July 1963**, Chief Justice Lett held the Power Development Act 1961, its 1962 amendment and the 1962 Hydro Act **ultra vires**. This report is contemporary primary corporate evidence of the judgment’s reported effect. **The full judgment and any separate appellate disposition have not been recovered in this package**, so the claim is expressly attributed to the report and makes no unsupported assertion about an appellate outcome. [R4E-033] (printed pp.17 and 22)

The same report describes the **27 September 1963 settlement agreement** following the share valuation, with compensation of **$197,114,358**, and the subsequent discharge of the receiver. It explains that BC Electric and the Power Commission continued conducting business together under the Hydro name. The later **Power Measures Act 1964** explicitly ratifies the September agreement and validates actions taken under the earlier arrangements. This is the reason not to treat operational continuity as proof that the original 1962 legal mechanism remained untouched. [R4E-033], [R4E-038]

### 1964 authority and retroactive acquisition law

Two 1964 statutes supply distinct legal functions:

* The **BC Electric Company Limited Acquisition Act**, c.6, assented **20 March 1964**, authorizes acquisition of all common shares **as at 1 August 1961**, for the stated compensation. Its retroactive effect should be recorded alongside, not substituted for, its actual enactment date. [R4E-034]
* The **BC Hydro and Power Authority Act 1964**, c.7, establishes a new statutory corporation, provides Crown agency and board appointment machinery, and authorizes amalgamation procedures. **OIC 773/1964** orders proclamation; **OIC 2519/1964**, recital 4, expressly records that the proclamation of **23 March 1964** brought the Act into effect. That combination supplies the precise legal-establishment date. [R4E-035], [R4E-036], [R4E-037]

The 1964 Act says the authority is a Crown agent, the Minister of Finance is its **fiscal agent**, and the Lieutenant Governor in Council appoints directors. Each is a distinct typed relationship. In particular, “Finance is fiscal agent” should not be generalized to “Hydro answers to Finance for every purpose” or used to fill a whole ministry-responsibility history. [R4E-035] (ss.3–6)

### The actual 1965 amalgamation and formal cancellation

**OIC 2386/1965**, approved **20 August 1965**, authorizes the three-party amalgamation of the **1964 Hydro authority**, **BC Electric Company Limited**, and **BC Power Commission**. Its procedure requires an agreement that supplies the effective date and time. The order therefore identifies the legal participants and approval, but the agreement must be read for actual effectiveness. [R4E-039]

The entire agreement is reproduced in the schedule to the **Power Measures Act 1966**, c.38. Clause 2 makes it effective **at 5 p.m. local Vancouver time on Friday, 20 August 1965**. Clause 1 continues the three as the Hydro authority established by the **1964 Act**, ends BC Electric’s and the Power Commission’s separate corporate existence, and carries the predecessor property, rights, powers, debts and obligations into that authority. Section 3 of the 1966 Act ratifies and confirms the agreement from August 20; the Act’s assent on **1 April 1966** is a later validating event, not the amalgamation date. [R4E-040] (ss.3,5,9 and Schedule)

**OIC 2387/1965**, approved **23 August 1965**, then separately applies the Companies Act machinery to revoke and cancel BC Electric’s incorporation and declare it dissolved. It expressly recites that the amalgamation has already occurred and the company has ceased separate existence. The atlas can therefore record both **August 20: separate existence ends through amalgamation**, and **August 23: formal cancellation/dissolution order**. The latter should not erase the former. [R4E-041]

The recommended model is a continuous public-service lineage with legally distinct dated events. It should keep the **1962 historical commencement**, add the **1964 legal constitution**, and end the two separately identified predecessors at the **1965 amalgamation**, with BC Electric’s subsequent formal dissolution also visible. This is an interpretation of how to represent the supplied documents, not a claim that every earlier organizational chart used these distinctions.

## 5. Vancouver Wharves: stronger identity boundaries, but the exact closing is still open

A primary public checkpoint remains **30 March 1993**. During oral questions, the Minister of Transportation and Highways responds to the recently completed BC Rail purchase of Vancouver Wharves. The exchange supports acquisition **on or before March 30**. It does not supply a precise closing date or the executed share-purchase agreement. [R4E-042]

A **July 1993** railway publication reprints an item expressly credited to BC Rail’s **Coupler**. It describes Vancouver Wharves as previously held by officers and directors, says it would remain a separate company with its own board and management, and notes that 98 of its 111 acres were already leased from BC Rail. This is useful contemporary corporate-announcement evidence for separate governance after the acquisition. Its transmission through a reprint and absence of a registry number mean it does not reconcile every later legal entity with a similar name. [R4E-043]

A later historical article, **Cariboo issue 30, October 1997, printed p.14**, gives **25 March 1993** as the acquisition date. That is retained in the structured file as an **unverified secondary candidate**, with `import_action: hold`. No contemporary closing document found in this continuation corroborates the day. [R4E-044]

For the later structure, **Ministerial Order 5/2004**, Railway Certificate 6497, is actually signed **30 December 2003**. Schedule A(j) describes BC Rail Ltd.’s **75% interest in Vancouver Wharves Limited Partnership** being transferred to BCRC or an affiliate as part of the approved restructuring. This improves the relationship type: it is a limited-partnership interest, not necessarily shares in a corporation called Vancouver Wharves. The ministerial approval and described future transactions do not alone prove the closing date. The 2004 numbering should also not be substituted for the document’s 2003 signature date. [R4E-045]

The remaining identity work concerns the separate registry records already flagged in the prior package, including numbers **44407, 59694, 176504, 1282745 and 284435**. Their incorporation, amalgamation and continuation events should not be flattened into one start/end pair until the legal bridges are recovered. The next decisive evidence is the 1993 BC Rail acquisition file/annual-report note, executed purchase agreement, share transfer, and the later partnership/general-partner reorganization instruments.

## 6. Other useful exact dates

### British Columbia Steamship Company

The first-year audited financial statements in Public Accounts 1975/76, **F84**, establish that **British Columbia Steamship Company (1975) Ltd.** was incorporated under the **Canada Corporations Act on 5 May 1975** to operate *Princess Marguerite*. On **15 May 1975**, it acquired **British Columbia Steamship Company Inc.**, a separate Washington corporation used for liquor transactions aboard the vessel in that state. The Canadian company’s share capital was held by **five directors in right of the Province**; the balance sheet lists five issued common shares. These are useful exact corporate and beneficial-ownership facts, not evidence that the directors owned it personally or proof of a ministry parent. [R4E-002] (F80–85)

The notes also distinguish the operator from **Crown Development Corporation**, whose books carried the vessel’s original refit costs and with which the operating company had an informal lease arrangement. A subsequent contemplated asset acquisition is described conditionally. It should not become a completed acquisition event without the later agreement or financial note. [R4E-002] (F84–85)

### British Columbia Harbours Board

The **Harbour Board Repeal Act**, SBC 1983, c.5, received assent **23 September 1983**, but its repeal and dissolution sections 2 and 3 required commencement by regulation. **OIC 1061/1985**, approved **30 May 1985**, brings those sections into force. Section 3 dissolves the board and terminates the members’ appointments when the old Act is repealed. Public Accounts 1984/85, **C24**, note 19(b), expressly confirms **30 May 1985**. This closes the specific E10 dissolution-date question with both original law and contemporary accounts. [R4E-047], [R4E-048], [R4E-017]

Sections 4 and 5 of the repeal Act separately provide for specified assets and liabilities to go to **BC Development Corporation**, and the remaining assets and liabilities to **BC Railway Company**, on transfer dates fixed by Cabinet. The dissolution order does not supply those preceding asset-transfer orders. The atlas can add the proved legal end now while keeping the exact transfer chronology as a separate gap. [R4E-047]

## 7. Integration rules and remaining work

The distinction the user wants between a body and its relationship to people depends on keeping the relationship evidence precise. This package’s `typed_relationships.json` separates share ownership, beneficial ownership, Crown agency, fiscal agency, statute administration, acquisition and board appointment. Those lines are explicitly **not** treated as democratic-accountability paths. The suggested accountability view still needs the relevant ministerial responsibility, legislative scrutiny, audit, complaint and remedy evidence from the other research packages.

For temporal data, preserve at least the following distinctions wherever the repository can support them:

1. **Assent / approval / signature:** the date the instrument was made.
2. **Commencement / transfer:** the date its operative event occurred.
3. **Retroactive or deemed date:** a legal date established later.
4. **Observation:** evidence that a relationship existed at a financial-statement or publication date, without claiming its first day.
5. **Legal identity / operational identity:** the company or statutory body can persist after a transfer of its operating functions.

The claims carry `date_kind`, precision, source IDs, locators, caveats, and a novelty classification. The resolution date for Pemco is new while the certificate is corroboration; the March 1993 Hansard is primary corroboration with a tighter latest-date bound; the Wharves day remains a held candidate. Baseline PRHC registry dates, Forest Renewal BC’s **27 June 2002** end, and the already proved BC Rail/BCR Properties registry chains are retained from the previous package and are not counted as new closures here.

## Evidence, retrieval and packaging notes

`sources.json` is the curated manifest. Every source has an original local path (`path` and `local_file`), original SHA-256, URL, review scope, source class and acceptance role. `package_evidence_path` points to the material intended for the final evidence bundle. Small instruments are exact downloaded originals; selected pages from the three large financial volumes and Hydro report retain the original page content and carry an explicit original-PDF-to-package-page map. The full original downloads remain available in `raw/`.

The two large Public Accounts originals are **different volumes**, 1974/75 and 1975/76, not duplicate copies. The discovery sweep duplicates the two 1978 Transit orders and the 1980 MTOC order. An earlier full-volume OAG package copy is also preserved in `raw/` with a `superseded-package-copy` prefix. These can be deduplicated by hash during packaging; the curated `evidence/` folder contains exactly the 48 files in the manifest. None of the preserved originals needs to be deleted.

`discovery_ledger.json` separates unsuccessful or unused downloads from accepted evidence. Two UBC download/API paths returned an explicit unusual-activity HTML page and were not used or bypassed; independently available Internet Archive scans supplied the volumes. A 2021 official liquor-history URL returned 503 on direct download and is not counted as a curated source. Empty OIC directory responses are likewise not evidence. The targeted Transit sweep text-screened 199 orders; only the relevant original instruments enter the curated source catalogue.

The files are research findings and proposed typed events, not automatic repository mutations. Exact-day entries can be imported where the identity is secure. The marked Wharves candidate and the explicit unresolved identity/commencement issues must remain qualified.
