# PofBC — Round 4 branches, programs and historical office families

Research checked **21 October 2026** against the held Round 3 package and its 33-record `remaining-undated.json` baseline. This is a research handback; no repository changes, publication or external correspondence were made.

## What this pass changes

**Twenty-one of the 33 previously undated records now have usable dated evidence: 16 from this research pass and five from integrating evidence already present in other Round 3 modules. Twelve remain without an accepted pre-2026 date.** This is a reduction in undated records, not a claim that 21 branch founding dates have been found. Some records describe statutory accounts, programs, public information resources or a campaign brand. Their entity types must be corrected before the dates are drawn as organizational history.

The strongest historical additions are:

- The **Medical Services Commission existed in March 1967**, with initial members appointed effective **24 March 1967** and an organizational staff establishment effective **25 March 1967**. Original orders distinguish this from the **1 July 1968** Medicare operating milestone used as an establishment date in later financial statements. The original appointment order is direct, dated evidence; the separate Gazette proclamation still needs recovery. [R4B-070–074, R4B-076, R4B-078]
- The **Parks and Recreation Division moved to the Department of Recreation and Conservation and became the Provincial Parks Branch on 1 April 1957**, according to the department’s contemporary annual report. That report also dates the Fish and Game and Photographic Branch transitions. The signed implementing instrument is still missing. [R4B-013]
- A contemporary **April–May 1980** article describes the Conservation Officer Service’s separation from the Fish and Wildlife Branch as already accomplished. A September 1980 order explicitly places COS employees in the Ministry of Environment, and the 1980/81 annual report locates the service in Regional Operations. The separation directive itself remains missing. [R4B-003, R4B-014, R4B-051]
- Original sheriff statutes and orders now supply a much stronger chain from the colonial High Sheriff through territorial offices, six county-name continuations in 1888, Vancouver appointments in 1892–93, the 1895 county scheme, Atlin in 1905 and Prince Rupert in 1920. These distinguish appointment authority, an actual appointment, a territorial change and a continuing office. They do **not** establish that nine county offices all began in 1860. [R4B-041, R4B-046, R4B-055, R4B-058–068]

The handback contains **88 claim records**. Four are explicitly classified as a conflicting retrospective statement, a rejected instrument candidate or archival leads; the rest are substantive historical findings, including nine claims integrating prior-module evidence. Claim count is not a count of independently created bodies or fully closed research questions. `claims.json` records each event’s meaning, date precision, source and locator. `coverage.json` gives the exact 33-record comparison.

## 2. Medical Services Commission: a recovered founding bundle

The original **Medical Services Act, 1967 c24** received assent on **23 March 1967**. Section3 constitutes the Commission; section13 leaves the substantive provisions to proclamation. The next-day date comes from the orders, not from treating assent as automatic commencement. [Original Act](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/hstats/hstats/30844302), printed pp111–115 / PDF pp1–5, particularly ss3 and13; R4B-070.

| Instrument | Approval | Effective event documented |
|---|---|---|
| **OIC 964/1967** | 23 March 1967 | Directs a proclamation bringing all provisions except s9 into force **24 March 1967**. This includes the constituting section. |
| **OIC 965/1967** | 23 March 1967 | Appoints **Geoffrey Alexander Stewart** as member and chairman, **Peter John Banks** and **Gerald Stewart Bryson** as members. Expressly effective **24 March 1967**. |
| **OIC 966/1967** | 23 March 1967 | Directs separate commencement of **s9 on 25 March 1967**, covering administrative services and employee transfers from specified medical-insurance carriers. |
| **OIC 967/1967** | 23 March 1967 | Sets the Commission’s listed civil-service organizational establishment effective **25 March 1967**. |
| **OIC 1518/1968** | May 1968 | Adds three listed positions effective **15 May 1968**. This independently confirms existence before July 1968. |

Sources: [964](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/oic/arc_oic/0964_1967), [965](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/oic/arc_oic/0965_1967), [966](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/oic/arc_oic/0966_1967), [967](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/oic/arc_oic/0967_1967), [1518](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/oic/arc_oic/1518_1968), each PDF p1. R4B-071–074, R4B-078. The first four scans were visually checked, including names and operative dates.

A **6 June 1968** full-page public announcement issued by the Commission and its chairman tells residents about upcoming **1 July** Medicare coverage. This is additional contemporary evidence that the Commission preceded the new plan’s operation. [Gulf Islands Driftwood](https://saltspringarchives.com/driftwood/1968/June6-1968.pdf), p11; R4B-075.

The Commission’s financial statement for the year ended **31 March 2004**, note1, instead describes establishment on **1 July 1968**. That is retained as a conflicting later statement. The original 1967 appointment and staff orders should govern the atlas’s earliest documented existence; the report does not prove the later wording was intended to describe legal constitution rather than operations. [Financial statement](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/health/practitioner-pro/medical-services-plan/bluebook2004.pdf), p6; R4B-076.

Two useful relationship distinctions follow. The original Act places member appointment with the Lieutenant Governor in Council, makes the Commission a Crown agent, gives Finance inspection rights, requires Comptroller-General audit, and routes the annual report through the designated minister to the Legislature. These are distinct appointment, financial scrutiny and reporting relationships. The **initial ministerial designation was not found**; a Provincial Secretary’s signature on an order is insufficient by itself. The 2004 statement also says the Financial Administration Act applies **as though the Commission were a division of the Ministry of Health Services**. That limited accounting rule should not become an ordinary “part of ministry” edge. [R4B-070, printed p112, ss6–8; R4B-076, p6.]

## 3. Parks, Game and Conservation Officers

The Department of Recreation and Conservation’s **1957 annual report** supplies an exact effective transition that was missing from the earlier legal-history outline. Its Parks history identifies the **1948 Parks and Recreation Division** predecessor and says it entered the new department on **1 April 1957**, becoming the **Provincial Parks Branch**. The Fish and Game history likewise gives **1 April 1957** for the former Game Commission’s move; the introduction identifies its former Attorney-General location. The Photographic Branch report expressly dates its becoming a separate unit to **1 April 1957**, having previously formed part of the Government Travel Bureau. [UBC digitized original](https://open.library.ubc.ca/collections/bcsessional/items/1.0354514); [original-report fulltext API](https://oc-index.library.ubc.ca/collections/bcsessional/items/1.0354514); R4B-013.

The preserved API response contains the report’s original OCR, not a modern summary. The introduction is printed **II7**; the Photographic statement is **II42**. The Parks history falls after the **II21** section cover and before photographs preceding **II24**. Because the PDF image could not be retrieved, an exact printed page number for that paragraph is deliberately not invented. This evidence supports the effective transfer date; the signed cabinet or administrative instrument remains open.

For COS, Andy Ackerman’s article in the Department of Fisheries and Oceans staff newsletter **The Sounder, volume VIII no.3, April–May 1980**, reports the service’s removal from the Fish and Wildlife Branch and its new enforcement role for the entire Ministry of Environment. It is a contemporaneous account by a BC conservation officer, not the missing directive. The issue date gives an observation window, not a day of reorganization. [Bound newsletter original](https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/Library/170964-1980.pdf), issue cover PDF p37; printed pp10–11 / PDF pp46–47; R4B-003.

[OIC 2085/1980](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/oic/arc_oic/2085_1980), approved **18 September 1980**, then confers statutory powers on listed COS employees of the Ministry of Environment. It concerns powers and designations, not organizational creation. The [1980/81 ministry report](https://oc-index.library.ubc.ca/collections/bcsessional/items/1.0372122), printed pp57–58, places COS in **Regional Operations Division** during its extended reporting period, 1 January 1980–31 March 1981. R4B-051 and R4B-014.

The exact Wildfire Management Branch renaming order and the Surveyor General’s 2019 internal division instrument were not recovered. Prior findings on the Wildfire name’s **6 June 2015** appearance, the 2005 LTSA transfer, EAB commencement and Powertech corporate dates are not being counted again as discoveries.

### The 1974 consolidation question

The new search **did not recover the administrative instrument that combined or reorganized the nine offices**. The earlier package’s 1973 planning, 1 April 1974 expansion of duties, 5 July training graduation and January 1975 staffing observations remain distinct milestones. The previously documented nine offices in eight counties must not become nine counties in the model.

Two new archival targets are now precisely identified by a contemporary government task-force working report:

1. **Frank Hodgson et al., Report of the Task Force on Sheriff Services, May 1977.**
2. **John Dogherty et al., Sheriff Services Program Inventory and Review, September 1977**, Court Services, Ministry of the Attorney General.

They are cited in the **July 1978 Taskforce on Municipal Policing Costs in British Columbia, Preliminary Report**, Appendix12, printed pp352–353 / PDF p239. The original 1977 reports were not recovered in this pass. The 1978 report’s own administrative description places Sheriff Services under the **Director of Court Services**, with regional sheriffs reporting to managers of **six justice regions**; this is a useful 1978 checkpoint, not the 1974 instrument. [Original scan hosted by the US Department of Justice](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/Digitization/88577NCJRS.pdf), printed p356 / PDF p242; R4B-035.

An attractive false lead can be rejected: on **6 June 1974**, the Attorney General expressly said that **Police Act Bill91 had nothing to do with the new sheriff service**. [Hansard](https://api.lims.leg.bc.ca/hdms/file/Debates/30th4th/30p_04s_740606z.htm), p3809; R4B-011. OIC562/1974 was also screened and concerns affidavit commissioners, not the consolidation.

For earlier individual offices, the **1890 Sheriffs Amendment Act, s6** directs publication each July in the Gazette of every sheriff’s full name, postal address and bailiwick limits. This creates a specific serial-research route. The Act’s own commencement order and the year-by-year Gazette rosters are still missing. [Original 1890 c45](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/hstats/hstats/659866514), p244 / PDF p2, ss6–7; R4B-065.

## 5. Relationship semantics and historical placement

The new dates do not justify filling every earlier or later year with today’s parent. Useful new checkpoints include:

- **Buy BC:** BC Agricultural Council administration in 2002; Food and Beverage Branch → Agriculture Resource Division → Ministry of Agriculture and Food in the June 2023 audit. [R4B-018, pp3–4.]
- **Provincial Treasury:** Ministry of Finance in the 1986 Auditor General report; Ministry of Finance and Corporate Relations in the 29 July 1996 estimates debate. [R4B-027; [Hansard](https://api.lims.leg.bc.ca/hdms/file/Debates/36th1st/19960729pm2-Hansard-v2n7.htm), p1077, R4B-002.]
- **Building and Safety Standards Branch:** an intervening Office of Housing and Construction Standards and the named 2014 ministry, not the current parent projected backward. [R4B-030, p3.]
- **Infrastructure divisions:** explicit organizational placement as of 17 March 2025. [R4B-006, pp17–18.]
- **Disaster Financial Assistance:** the contemporary 2016 archived release identifies Emergency Management BC and the Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure. The modern news website’s current navigation labels are not evidence of that historical parent. [R4B-042.]
- **Trans Care BC:** expressly a PHSA program from fall 2015. [R4B-052, p3.]
- **Building BC:** program administration by BC Housing, not direct ministry-branch membership. [R4B-007, p5.]

The Arts Council’s role in the BC Arts and Culture Endowment is **recommending payments**, specifically new s9(5)(b), not ownership. Finance/GCPE’s role in the Let’s Go BC official mark is **formal rights holding**, not proof of campaign administration. The Medical Services Commission’s “as though a division” rule is **financial-administration treatment**, not a general organizational identity. These are concrete reasons for the atlas to support more than one edge type. [R4B-022, pp42–43; R4B-049, p2673; R4B-076, p6.]

The Governor General’s register adds a distinct sheriff mark milestone: the arms, supporters, flag and badge were granted **15 August 2011**, with Gazette announcement **24 March 2012**. This is a heraldic grant date, not first use of every sheriff logo or permission to reproduce it. [Public register, volumeVI p64](https://www.gg.ca/en/heraldry/public-register/project/2209), R4B-020.

## 6. Evidence preservation, limitations and next targeted work
