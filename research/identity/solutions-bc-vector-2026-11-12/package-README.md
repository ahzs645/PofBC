## How it was reconstructed

The emblem uses two deliberately drawn figure shapes. Each is reused with a 180-degree rotation for its opposite figure. Four circles form the heads. This preserves the small difference between the upright and sideways figures in the supplied image, instead of imposing exact 90-degree symmetry that the reference does not have.

Straight sections and a small set of cubic and quadratic curves define the figure silhouettes. No bitmap tracing or contour-to-path conversion was used.

Both text lines are custom letter outlines. Repeated letters share the same authored glyph designs, with small placement and scale adjustments to match the supplied layout. The full SVG contains 30 closed path instances and four circles. The background edition adds one rectangle. There are no embedded raster images, external font files, or live SVG text elements.

The original historical font has not been identified or authenticated. The lettering is a reconstruction of the supplied image, rather than a claim that the original typeface has been recovered.

Colours were sampled and normalized from the supplied raster. They are not an independently verified historical brand specification. Texture and small lighting variations in that raster have been replaced by flat vector fills.

## Comparison and validation

The reconstruction was rendered at the source resolution and compared using filled silhouette masks. These masks were used only for evaluation; they did not generate vector paths. Intersection-over-union is the shared filled area divided by the total filled area covered by either version.

| Region | Filled-shape intersection-over-union |
| --- | ---: |
| Emblem | 98.30% |
| Solutions BC wordmark | 94.71% |
| Shared Services tagline | 91.24% |
| All artwork | 97.71% |

These figures measure silhouette agreement with this specific raster. They are not a statement of historical authenticity. Remaining differences are mostly thin edge regions, the normalized curved arms, letter terminals, and uneven edges in the supplied image. The smaller lettering is more sensitive to a one-pixel edge change than the large emblem.

The SVGs were parsed and rendered to check structure, letter counters, spacing, margins, and transparency. The PDF was reopened, rendered, and checked: one page, 35 vector drawing objects, zero image objects, and zero font objects. An independent visual review found no material clipping or design defects.

## Editing

The SVG artwork is grouped into `emblem`, `wordmark`, and `tagline`; individual paths and heads also have descriptive IDs. Open an SVG in a vector editor to recolour, scale, or adjust individual components. The text is editable as outlines rather than as typed characters.

To rebuild the SVGs after changing `source/geometry.json`, run:

```sh
python source/rebuild_vectors.py
```

This creates a `rebuilt` folder beside this document. It does not require the original image, an installed typeface, or third-party Python packages. PNG previews, the PDF, and comparison measurements are not regenerated by this script.
