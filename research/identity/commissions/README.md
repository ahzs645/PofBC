# B.C. commissions

The gallery’s Commissions category brings together 35 historical and continuing bodies. It covers every commission named in the project’s provincial atlas and selected additional bodies documented in statutes and public reports. The BC Treaty Commission, for example, was established jointly by the First Nations Summit, Canada and B.C. The public status of each body depends on its own mandate.

## Where to find commissions

- The Province’s directory gives mandates, structure and appointments for public organizations, including commissions. [Directory of Organizations](https://www.bcpublicsectorboardapplications.gov.bc.ca/s/directoryofagencies)
- For time-limited public inquiries, the Legislative Library offers a searchable collection beginning in 1872 and checklists for 1872–1942, 1943–1980 and 1981–2005. Some reports are missing. The portal omits commissions terminated before completing their work. [Royal and special commissions](https://www.leg.bc.ca/learn/legislative-library/library-publications/library-royal-commissions)
- For former government agencies, the provincial archives preserve records and administrative histories. [BC Archives government records](https://bcarchives.ca/government-records/)

## Where the Alcohol and Drug Commission’s work went

| Date | What changed |
| --- | --- |
| 1973 | The legislature created the Commission as a provincial Crown agent. [Founding Act, ss. 2–4](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/hstats/hstats/408463782) |
| 1979 revision | The supplied Act continued the corporation with at least three provincially appointed members. It could operate or fund treatment, research and public education programs and had to report annually to the minister. [Revised Act, ss. 2, 4, 10–11](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/hstats/hstats/333133237) |
| By October 3, 1983 | Health Minister Jim Nielsen told the legislature that the Ministry of Health had integrated the program over the preceding two years. Alcohol and Drug Programs had absorbed its staff, and the Commission had become advisory. [Hansard, pp. 2307–08](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/hdms/file/Debates/33rd1st/33p_01s_831003p.htm#02307) |
| October 26, 1983 | The repeal of the Commission’s Act took effect. [Repeal Act](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/hstats/hstats/577115892) · [Official commencement table](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/tos/tos/973948705) |
| 1996/97 | The Province moved Alcohol and Drug Services from Health to the Ministry for Children and Families. [Financial and Economic Review](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/government-finances/financial-economic-review/financial-economic-review-1997.pdf) |
| By April 2002 | The programs returned to Health Services. The Province transferred adult alcohol and drug services to regional health authorities in April. [Hansard, April 3](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/hdms/file/Debates/37th3rd/20020403pm-Hansard-v5n6.htm) · [Supreme Court of Canada, 2011 SCC 44, para. 14](https://bccla.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/03/20110930-Supreme-Court-Ruling-PHS.pdf) |
