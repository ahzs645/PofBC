## Identifying the held PDF precisely

The checksum-verified PDF records the following metadata, independently read back
from the retained file:

- Creator: **Adobe Acrobat 11.0**.
- Producer: **Adobe Acrobat Pro 11.0.0 Paper Capture Plug-in**.
- PDF creation timestamp: **4 May 2016, 14:41:06, UTC-07:00**.
- Cover page: approximately **522.960 x 758.880 PDF points**.
- Embedded cover JPEG: **2179 x 3162 pixels**, placed at approximately **300 pixels
  per PDF inch** in both dimensions.

The timestamp is PDF metadata; it does not independently establish when the
physical cover was scanned. The image-placement density is calculated from native
pixels and PDF coordinates; it is not a separate measurement of the physical
original or its optical resolution. The image hash, extraction provenance and
native crop coordinates remain in [registration.json](registration.json).

## Specific acquisition target

Seek the original digitization master used for **the illustrated brown-ink front
cover of _Provincial Museum of Natural History and Anthropology: Report for the
Year 1960_, printed in 1961**, especially the small arms below its title. The PDF
metadata above helps the Museum identify the relevant digital file.

A native TIFF or JP2 may preserve better tonal or compression information, but
its pixel dimensions must be checked before calling it higher resolution. If no
better master exists, a new optical scan of the physical cover, retaining colour
and unsharpened native pixels, is the useful next source. Approximately 1200 dpi
would be a practical request for this small mark; any gain must come from scanning
the object rather than interpolation. The current scan cannot settle eyes,
animal anatomy, hatching or exact lettering through enlargement alone.

Keep the 1954 cover, 1954 title page and 1978 headings as separately identified
specimens. A better scan of a different engraving cannot authenticate this one.
