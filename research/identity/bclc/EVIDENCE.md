# Evidence and remaining gaps

Checked 27 September 2026. Source numbers match the timeline.

## What is confirmed

**[1] Legacy full-name lockup, 2006/07.** The annual report cover shows the sun-and-waves emblem with the full corporate name. This provides a dated use of the older family, not its first appearance.

**[2] Legacy lockup, early 2008.** The cover of the 2008/09–2010/11 service plan still carries the older full-name identity. The plan's multi-year end date is not the logo date: this is an early-2008 document.

**[3] Major redesign, August 2008.** Canadian Press's article dated 13 August 2008 describes the new lowercase BCLC acronym, inset coloured circles and the “playing it right” tagline. The article explains that the wider brand reflects a business beyond lottery products. This is a public-facing branding change; the timeline does not assert a legal corporate name change.

**[4] New family, 2009.** The 2009/10–2011/12 service-plan cover visibly uses the new lowercase identity, with red, orange and green circles. The 2008 and 2009 covers corroborate the contemporary report's chronology.

**[5] Award, 2011.** Printed page 6 (PDF page 10, one-based) of the 2010/11 annual report states that BCLC received recognition at the 2011 REBRAND 100 Global Awards for its brand transformation. That year should not be used to date the original lowercase logo launch.

**[6] Older palette continues in a 2022 document.** The ESG report cover shows the red/orange/green dot palette. This is a document-specific use-date. It does not prove a universal end date or exclude concurrent use of another palette.

**[7] Older digital colour guide.** BCLC's design-system page lists green #7DBC13, orange #E86A10, red #9B1831 and grey #414B56. These values support a transparent editorial reconstruction of the older palette. The page does not establish the original 2008 print specification or the exact date these hex values were adopted. Its footer supplies the corporation's “since 1985” operating-history context; that is not independent proof of a logo introduction date.

**[8] Contemporary colour family, 2025/26.** Page 1 of the official Prince George Community Impact Report visibly carries a lowercase logo with purple, coral and yellow inset circles. It confirms recent dated use. The supplied SVG's exact RGB values are retained independently of the PDF's print/display rendering. The 2025/26 label refers to the report, not a verified launch window.

**[9] Gradual rollout.** The 2008 Carbon Neutral Action Report, dated June 30, 2009, describes a soft rollout: supplies bearing the new logo replaced existing stock as that stock was exhausted. This cautions against dating physical signs or stationery solely from the formal redesign date.

## What was not established

1. The first appearance, designer and full-colour master specification of the supplied legacy sun-and-waves lockup.
2. The exact launch date of the purple/coral/yellow palette.
3. An original red/orange/green vector master that would permit an exact contour comparison with the supplied contemporary SVG.
4. The original tagline font and its master spacing/proportions.
5. An exhaustive sequence of all stacked, horizontal, reversed, monochrome, signage and tagline applications.

These gaps are represented as uncertainty in the artwork and the JSON data, rather than filled with assumed years. The two supplied logo designs support two broad structural families in this reference set. This does not prove that no additional historic variants exist.

## Treatment of the uploaded ZIP

`BCLC/before/before.svg` supplies four compound paths: three symbol paths plus the lettering path. The large source background and extraneous document clip were removed for the normalized positive/reversed assets. The supplied wording, letter contours and emblem shapes were not traced or re-typeset.

`BCLC/curent.svg` supplies the lowercase path geometry, three inset circle paths and exact contemporary hex values. Solid rectangular masks were converted to equivalent vector clips in normalized outputs to keep PDF exports entirely vector-based. Original source files are preserved.

The two building/signage screenshots do not establish the age of the photographed sign. A screenshot's September 2026 capture timestamp is not an installation, photo or logo-introduction date. Different line arrangements in signage can be lockup variants rather than different eras.
