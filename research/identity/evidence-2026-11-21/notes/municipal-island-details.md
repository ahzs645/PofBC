## Findings that change the evidence classification

### 1. shíshálh Nation Government District: actual District use of shared artwork is visually documented

The official [Business License Application](https://shishalh.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/sNGD-Business-License-Form.pdf), PDF page 1, displays a coloured crest directly beside the name “shishalh Nation Government District.” This is direct use-by-District evidence. It is stronger than finding a Nation crest on the shared website alone.

I downloaded the original, rendered page 1 and visually compared the crest to page 1 of the Nation’s [2021 annual report](https://shishalh.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/SN-2021-Annual-Report_FINAL.pdf). They show the same central face, broad black wings, yellow and red upper details, and blue lower motif. The annual report explicitly identifies shíshálh Nation.

**Recommended status:** `shared artwork; District use verified; exclusive District design unconfirmed`. This does not establish a separate District logo, but it also should not remain a blanket “cannot attribute use to the District” result. Keep the subject and the shared artwork relationship separately. Whether this counts as accepted gallery artwork depends on whether the collection admits documented shared use; it should not be presented as a newly discovered exclusive mark.

**Date qualifications:** the form is undated on its printed page. Its URL is in a 2023/09 upload directory; PDF creation metadata says 14 October 2022 and modification metadata says 19 October 2022. Neither proves first use. The annual report is explicitly labelled 2021, with PDF creation metadata in November 2021 and a 2022/01 upload path. These are distinct observations, not adoption dates.

**Original formats:** the form embeds a 158 × 109 RGB raster crest at image xref 166. The Nation’s report cover appears to hold the crest as PDF vector artwork; its only embedded raster on that page is a 750 × 1414 CMYK landscape photograph. Vector appearance is supported by the PDF structure and the rendered page, but extracting and checking a standalone vector was outside this research pass.

### 2. Okanagan Falls: dated evidence of branding still to be developed

[Levi Landry’s iNFOnews report, 4 November 2026](https://infonews.ca/news/7974212/a-new-town-is-being-born-in-the-okanagan-heres-how-they-made-it/), expressly states that the new municipality had no logo yet. This is a contemporaneous local-news assertion, not an official branding-policy statement. It is more informative than an unsuccessful logo search, and it is tightly dated.

The [official website](https://okanaganfalls.ca/) still describes itself as the future official site. The [RDOS transition page](https://rdosregionalconnections.ca/district-okanagan-falls) and [inaugural-election page](https://rdosregionalconnections.ca/district-okanagan-falls/district-okanagan-falls-inaugural-election-2026) provide the current administrative source path. The [BC Gazette notice](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/bcgaz1/bcgaz1/470736345) gives incorporation effective 6 November 2026; this is useful for separating the new municipality from earlier community/tourism branding.

**Recommended status:** `municipal branding not yet documented; reported not adopted as of 4 November 2026`. Do not turn the news report into a timeless claim that the municipality has no logo. A later council decision could change it. Review official council agendas/website updates for a future adoption.

**False-positive to exclude:** the [South Okanagan Chamber page](https://www.sochamber.ca/okanagan-falls) contains `Okanagan_Falls_Logo.png`, but its text describes Okanagan Falls as an unincorporated Area D community governed by RDOS. That image is not evidence of adoption by the District established in 2026.



### 3. Grand Forks: strong source identified, use-permission question remains separate

The City’s [Logo Use Guidelines and User Agreement](https://www.grandforks.ca/wp-content/uploads/Logo-Use-Guidelines-and-User-Agreement.pdf) is a direct official source. Its parsed text identifies an eight-page PDF containing brand-book excerpts, use specifications, and an agreement. It says organizations should apply and receive permission before use, and describes submitting a visual proof. It also says AI and EPS vector originals exist. The [Communications Policy and Procedures](https://www.grandforks.ca/wp-content/uploads/Policy0206Communications-Policy-and-Procedures.pdf) identifies the logo as the official community symbol and refers to the Brand Guidelines adopted in 2013.

**Recommended status:** `official artwork/source confirmed; reproduction permission pending under the project’s existing handling`. Keep this separate from genuinely missing artwork. This report does not give a legal conclusion about archival display rights or contact the City. No copy was put in a gallery.

**Access and inspection:** search indexed/parsed text was accessible, but local download returned HTTP 403. The search screenshot operation returned image placeholders without usable image bytes. I therefore do not claim an independent full visual inspection of the Grand Forks guideline in this pass.



## Vancouver Island health-council work

### Additional journal lead: combined Campbell River/Nootka council

A Canadian Nurse recruitment advertisement names **Campbell River/Nootka Community Health Council** and directs applications to Arlene Rommel, Director of Human Resources, 375 – 2nd Avenue, Campbell River. The indexed attachment filename is **CN_IC_VOL_95_NO_2_FEB_1999.pdf**, establishing the issue as volume 95, number 2, February 1999. The object identifier is `CNA/14a28dd6-4d28-8a08-8812-7b292bbb98b1_file.pdf`.

The stable public starting point is the [Canadian Nurse archive](https://www.canadian-nurse.com/archive). Search-result signed download links had expired and returned HTTP 403, so the advertisement page image and printed page number remain unverified. Preserve the object identifier and issue date as retrieval aids; do not store the expired signed URL as a durable citation.

**Critical owner distinction:** this names the combined Campbell River/Nootka body, not the earlier **Campbell River and Area Health Council** or the separate **Nootka Community Health Council** in the missing list. It cannot close either of those earlier subjects without separately dated evidence. The combined body might already be covered in the full register.



### Southern Gulf Islands: original newspaper pages inspected, but no council artwork present

1. [Gulf Islands Driftwood, 10 April 1996](https://saltspringarchives.com/driftwood/1996/37n15Apr10-1996.pdf), printed page 12 / PDF page 12. The article about the community-awareness group names SGICHC, outlines its committees and interim chief executive, and says it reported to the Capital Health Board. The page was downloaded, rendered and visually inspected. The council appears in ordinary editorial text; surrounding business/school/Legion advertisements do not belong to the council.
2. [Gulf Islands Driftwood, 18 December 1996](https://saltspringarchives.com/driftwood/1996/V37n51Dec18-1996.pdf), printed page 2 / PDF page 2. The original article reports that the council was dissolved on 29 November 1996. This is contemporary reporting rather than the legal dissolution instrument. The page was visually inspected; no SGICHC logo appears.
3. The same 18 December issue, printed page 6 / PDF page 6, has a hospital response to loss of the council. Visually inspected; no council logo appears.

These pages narrow the useful search period and clarify ownership, but do not establish that the council never had a mark. Search council-issued letters, meeting notices or 1995–96 annual material instead of assigning the later Capital Health Region mark.
