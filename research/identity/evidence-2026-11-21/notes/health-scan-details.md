# PofBC historical health artwork research: exact-scan leads

Research date: 21 November 2026. Research scope: Thompson Health Region and Sea to Sky Community Health Council, with closely related archival sources.

## Result

**One distinct organizational mark visually verified: Thompson Health Region.** The successful source is a contemporary signed letter in a municipal agenda, not the originally suggested Canadian Nurse advertisement. Sea to Sky remains an unverified artwork lead; the scan host changed, and OCR text alone is insufficient.

No GitHub files were changed. This is a research evidence report, not a claim that the repository's accepted-artwork count has changed.

## 1. Thompson Health Region: confirmed visual evidence

### Primary source

- Document: Letter from Bettie Hall, Chair, THR Board of Governors, to Mayor Clara Norgaard, City of Merritt.
- Letter date: **9 September 1999**.
- Subject: Review of Nicola Valley Health Services.
- Containing document: City of Merritt regular council agenda, **12 October 1999**.
- Location: **PDF page 118 of 132**, handwritten agenda page **116**.
- Working public PDF URL: https://old.merritt.ca/wp-content/uploads/archives/meetings/1999/1999-10-12%20RC%20agenda.pdf
- Former indexed URL (now live 404): https://www.merritt.ca/wp-content/uploads/archives/meetings/1999/1999-10-12%20RC%20agenda.pdf
- City archive landing page: https://old.merritt.ca/virtual-city-archives/

### What was actually observed

The letterhead has a **black cross-shaped symbol containing a white winding path and small upright white forms near the top**, beside a serif name, **Thompson Health Region**, and italic strapline, **A Path to Better Health**. It is plainly a distinct organizational symbol in an official letterhead, rather than merely a name in a list. The precise intended meaning of the upright forms was not documented in the sources examined.

The mark is black and white in this scan. This source does not establish the original brand colours, launch date, designer, font identity, or technical construction. The scan is visibly degraded at edges; preserve it as a source specimen before attempting any vector reconstruction.

## 2. Original Canadian Nurse lead: corroborated in OCR; page images still unavailable

### October 2000

- Source: *Canadian Nurse*, October 2000, Vol. 96, No. 9, printed page **44**.
- Advertisement headline: Nursing Jobs at Four-season Recreation Centre.
- Indexed text contains the BC Thompson recruitment advertisement and the name/strapline **Thompson Health Region / A Path to Better Health**.
- Public archive entry: https://www.canadian-nurse.com/archive/2000-2009
- PDF object: `CNA/d6fbd830-ec32-7e05-e18b-ab0edc42edbf_file.pdf` on the official Higher Logic Canadian Nurse download host.
- Search provided an expired signed URL (2026-02-08 with one-hour lifetime). The cached OCR was readable, but fresh PDF screenshot/download attempts returned 403. Do not use the expiring query string as the permanent citation; retain issue/date/page and archive landing page.

### Additional corroboration: February 2001

- Source: *Canadian Nurse*, February 2001, Vol. 97, No. 2, printed page **42**.
- Same Thompson recruitment headline and name/strapline appear in indexed OCR.
- PDF object: `CNA/feffc54d-a302-ed72-cb02-f3b4389e3a5e_file.pdf`.
- This is an additional contemporary lead, not a visually verified reproduction.
- The issue also contains recruitment advertisements for Simon Fraser Health Region (printed p.37) and Fraser Valley Health Region (around p.39), plus South Fraser later in the issue. These are useful retrieval targets, but artwork remains unverified until page images are inspected.

### False-match warning

Do not import logos of **David Thompson Health Region, Alberta**. Search results heavily favour that distinct organization, its DTHR mountain/roof logo, and later Alberta Health Services branding. They do not belong to Thompson Health Region in BC.

## 3. Sea to Sky Community Health Council: exact current issue located; target page still unverified

### Exact original lead

- Newspaper: *The Chief*, Squamish, **8 May 2001**, printed **B12**.
- Indexed former PDF URL: https://squamishlibrary.digitalcollections.ca/uploads/r/squamish-public-library/2/2/22781/20010508_The_Chief.pdf
- OCR confirms a recruitment notice for a full-time social worker at Squamish General Hospital and Hilltop House, under the council's name. A second medical laboratory assistants notice also names the council.
- This does **not** establish whether either notice includes a distinct mark.

### Why the PDF is failing

The old URL now returns a small Artefactual fallthrough HTML application, not a PDF. The Squamish Public Library's current local-history page directs readers to **BiblioBoard**. Its 2024 provincial library grant report explicitly documents migration of all digital archival materials to BiblioBoard in early 2024.

- Current library page: https://squamishlibrary.ca/local-history/
- Current archive collection: https://library.biblioboard.com/curation/squamish-history-archives
- Official migration report: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/residents/sports-and-recreation/arts-culture/public-libraries/public-library-grant-reports/2024/plgr_-_squamish_public_library_-_2024.pdf

Initial web retrieval exposed only the client-rendered application loading page, and an unauthenticated API request returned 401. A later public-browser follow-up successfully reached catalogue search and the first page of the exact issue without login; see the follow-up section below. The library-linked curation itself currently renders 404 in this browser. These initial access failures therefore do not establish that the whole archive requires an account. No login, credential collection, or access-control workaround was attempted.

### What was directly observed

1. The official library-linked curation URL rendered an explicit 404 page.
2. The BiblioBoard public homepage loaded normally. Searching for Squamish returned 4,024 catalogue items, including Chief issues. No authentication prompt appeared.
3. Searching for `Squamish Chief May 08 2001` displayed the exact issue near the top. This broad search appears to match terms loosely; do not interpret its full result count as exact matches.
4. Clicking the issue metadata link returned a generic site error ("Oh no, what happened?!"). One reload returned the same error.
5. Returning to catalogue results and switching to **List View** exposed a **Read** link for the exact issue. That link successfully opened a real scan of the front page. The masthead and printed date **Tuesday, May 8, 2001** were visually inspected; this is stronger than an OCR-only date match.
6. The reader reported **page 1 of 3**. Requesting its next page produced the error **"This page failed to load. Please contact support if the error persists."** The view subsequently returned to catalogue results. There was no B12 page image available through the observed controls.
7. No separate B12 image, complete issue PDF, or council artwork was downloaded during this follow-up. Do not describe the target advertisement as visually inspected.

No account was created, no sign-in attempted, no credentials reused or extracted, and no access restrictions bypassed.
