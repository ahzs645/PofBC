## Main finding

**Thompson Health Region has a distinct mark that can now be inspected and attributed to a contemporary official letter.** The best recovered specimen is a 9 September 1999 letter preserved in Merritt's council records. The original Canadian Nurse lead is no longer the only route to its artwork.

There are also worthwhile improvements for BC Archives, BC Harbours Board, Sea to Sky, and the three municipal cases. They have different implications: a historical website masthead, documented shared provincial arms, a precise migrated newspaper record, and better distinctions between permission, shared use and branding still being developed.

These are research findings and proposed classifications. **The repository still has its recorded 108 gaps; this pass did not import assets or change the accepted-artwork count.** If the Thompson archival specimen is accepted, that alone would reduce the count to 107. BC Archives requires a separate decision about whether historical website mastheads qualify.

## 1. Thompson Health Region: the actionable recovery

The [Merritt agenda of 12 October 1999](https://old.merritt.ca/wp-content/uploads/archives/meetings/1999/1999-10-12%20RC%20agenda.pdf) contains a **9 September 1999 letter from Bettie Hall, Chair of the THR Board of Governors**, on **PDF page 118 of 132**, numbered **116** in the agenda.

Its letterhead displays a black cross-shaped symbol with a white winding path, the name **Thompson Health Region**, and **“A Path to Better Health.”** Both the page and extracted crop were visually inspected. This is organization-issued letterhead, giving substantially better attribution than an index entry or an adjacent advertisement.

The evidence package contains the one-page PDF, full-page rendering, letterhead crop and symbol crop. They preserve the scan without invented detail. The source does not establish original colours, exact typeface, designer, introduction date, or a clean vector master.

An earlier [24 September 1998 letter](https://old.merritt.ca/wp-content/uploads/archives/meetings/1998/1998-11-10%20RC%20agenda.pdf), PDF page 60 of the November 10 agenda, uses a typographic **THOMPSON REGIONAL HEALTH BOARD** header. That single sample cannot date the later emblem's introduction.

**Practical archive repair:** these actual Merritt PDF links work on `old.merritt.ca`; corresponding `www.merritt.ca` links now return 404. The [official old archive](https://old.merritt.ca/virtual-city-archives/) is a productive source for council correspondence.

- [Public reader](https://library.biblioboard.com/viewer/f3636ead-1fe6-4589-9de7-2af8b40610a8)

## 3. Municipal subjects: three different outcomes

### Grand Forks

The City's [Logo Use Guidelines and User Agreement](https://www.grandforks.ca/wp-content/uploads/Logo-Use-Guidelines-and-User-Agreement.pdf) require organizations to apply for permission and provide a visual proof of the proposed use. The document also identifies AI/EPS originals. This is a confirmed source and permissions workflow, rather than an unidentified logo. Direct downloading returned 403 in this pass, so no fresh full visual inspection is claimed. Preserve the project's current source-linked handling.

### shíshálh Nation Government District

The official [District business-licence form](https://shishalh.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/sNGD-Business-License-Form.pdf) places the coloured emblem beside the District's exact name. Comparison with the [Nation's 2021 annual-report cover](https://shishalh.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/SN-2021-Annual-Report_FINAL.pdf) confirms the same central face, wings and coloured details.

This strengthens the existing **shared-use** conclusion. It does not recover a District-exclusive design. Keep **subject using artwork** separate from **exclusive owner of a distinct design**. The form's printed page is undated; its 2023/09 upload path and October 2022 PDF metadata do not establish first use. The Nation's report supplies a potential higher-quality comparison source. The gallery's existing source-linked, unheld status remains unchanged; this comparison does not propose counting a District-exclusive recovery.

### Okanagan Falls

[Levi Landry's iNFOnews article of 4 November 2026](https://infonews.ca/news/7974212/a-new-town-is-being-born-in-the-okanagan-heres-how-they-made-it/) explicitly reports that the municipality had no logo yet. This is a dated local-news statement; the [official site](https://okanaganfalls.ca/) also remained a future-site placeholder when checked.

Use **“reported no municipal logo yet, 4 November 2026”**, while retaining uncertainty about later adoption. A pre-incorporation community or tourism image on a Chamber website is not evidence of District adoption.

## 4. Other identities: what the sources actually establish

### BC Archives: an authentic web masthead

The [23 January 1998 official-homepage capture](https://web.archive.org/web/19980123160726/http://www.bcarchives.gov.bc.ca/index.htm) references `/general/icons/archives2.gif`. The recovered [image capture is dated 10 June 2000](https://web.archive.org/web/20000610105838im_/http://www.bcarchives.gov.bc.ca/general/icons/archives2.gif). The original 543 × 67 GIF renders **British Columbia Archives** in shaded lettering. It was downloaded and visually inspected.

Describe it as **“historical official-site masthead; bitmap preserved in a 10 June 2000 capture.”** The earlier HTML proves the path existed, not that the recovered pixels are unchanged from 1998. General corporate-logo status remains unproven. The [June 2025 brochure](https://bcarchives.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/07/BC-Archives-Brochure-June-2025.pdf), inspected on both pages, uses Royal BC Museum branding and did not resolve a distinct Archives pictorial mark.

### BC Harbours Board: positive shared-branding evidence

The complete eight-page **Railway Time Table 5**, effective **24 April 1977**, was recovered from a [31 August 2022 archive capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20220831021633id_/http://wx4.org/to/foam/maps/1Perry4/1977-04-24BritishColumbiaHarboursBoardRailway5-SheldonPerry.pdf). Its cover places the provincial arms under the Board Railway's exact name. This is an original historical document scanned by a private railway-history collection.

A **6 October 1980** letter signed by Don Phillips as Minister and Harbours Board chairman provides corroboration. It appears in the Ombudsman's [The Cuthbert Case](https://bcombudsperson.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Special-Report-No-03-The-Cuthbert-Case.pdf), **PDF pp.47–48 / printed pp.43–44**, on provincial/ministry letterhead. Both pages were inspected.

Recommended status: **shared provincial identity documented**. Neither specimen establishes a distinct Board emblem or proves the arms were its separate corporate seal. An earlier archived timetable was truncated; only the complete 2022 copy was used.

### Metro Transit Operating Company

Museum of Vancouver item [H2015.37.548](https://openmovportal.ca/argus/final/Portal/Main.aspx?component=AAFG&record=039deb30-dd48-4139-b838-b7387660097b) is the **Spring 1983 Transit Exchange**, published for MTOC employees. The inspected cover carries the publication masthead and a safety-trophy photograph. The supplied image is too small to identify a company symbol on the trophy.

This is an exact follow-up accession, not an MTOC corporate-logo recovery. The issue's interior imprint, back cover and corporate notices are stronger next targets. The Buzzer and Transit Exchange titles should not be silently relabelled as company logos.

### BC Electric, Crown period

Museum of Vancouver [A-token item H2016.18.3](https://openmovportal.ca/argus/final/Portal/Main.aspx?component=AAFG&record=42752437-ccbf-4329-9b85-8f43bfbbd00c) and [B-token item H2015.37.13](https://openmovportal.ca/argus/final/Portal/Main.aspx?component=AAFG&record=233138fa-2875-46c8-a9af-9a8ef8b6727f) document inherited BC Electric branding. The museum explains that A/B tokens continued after 1962 to use existing stock. Both images were inspected, but their catalogue date range is **1960–1970**, crossing the ownership transition.

This supports continuity of token branding under public operation. It does not supply a clean corporate mark independently dated to the narrow Crown-company period. Seek a dated 1961–1962 letterhead, advertisement or report.

### Pacific Vehicle Testing Technologies

The [2010 TransLink annual report](https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/about-translink/corporate-reports/translink_annual_report/tl_annual_report_2010.pdf), PDF pp.8 and 13, shows the PVTT/AirCare relationship through a text governance box and program coverage. Those spreads were inspected. The [2014 report](https://www.translink.ca/-/media/translink/documents/about-translink/corporate-reports/translink_annual_report/tl_annual_report_2014.pdf) corroborates the relationship. A reproduced repair manual is a further text lead, not a recovered official artwork master. **A separate PVTT corporate mark remains unresolved.**

### Provincial Rental Housing Corporation

The [official organizational description](https://www.bchousing.org/about/our-organization) and [2023 corporation-specific financial statement](https://www.bchousing.org/publications/Provincial-Rental-Housing-Corporation-Financial-Statement-2023.pdf) support the BC Housing administrative context. The financial statement was already reviewed in the prior repository pass; this pass recovered no new distinct artwork. Preserve the existing shared-identity status. Architect logos and affidavit seals in property packets are not PRHC artwork.

### W.L.C. Developments

The [Land and Water BC 2003/04 audited statements](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/government-finances/public-accounts/2003-04/sup-e/land-and-water-bc-fs-2003-04.pdf) date incorporation under W.L.C. Developments to **5 January 1983** and the change to BC Assets and Land Corporation to **1 June 1998**. This gives a precise first search window for original letterheads, accounts and executed seals. No new attributable mark was recovered. The Whistler Resort Association's branding and later successor marks do not automatically belong to W.L.C.

## Recommended order for the next acquisition batch

1. **Review and ingest the Thompson source specimen.** Preserve the original page and crop; record the 1999 usage date, agenda page mapping and source hash. Treat any future vector tracing as a derivative.
2. **Decide the BC Archives masthead category.** If the gallery admits historical website graphics, keep that explicit description and the actual 2000 image-capture date.
3. **Improve evidence classifications without inventing independent logos.** Add the Harbours shared-branding specimens; retain the shíshálh shared-use treatment, Grand Forks permission state, and dated Okanagan Falls finding.
4. **Pursue the exact blocked records.** Sea to Sky's current issue/B12 and MTOC's museum accession offer concrete requests for missing source pages. No contact was sent in this pass.
5. **Continue by period and issuing organization.** Prioritize actual letters, annual reports and recruitment notices. Distinguish regional boards, later regions, combined councils and services societies even when they served similar areas.

## Package contents and provenance

The accompanying ZIP contains this report, an illustrated PDF, the unchanged 108-subject baseline, a structured research ledger, sanitized detailed research notes, and selected evidence pages/images. `evidence-manifest.json` records source URLs, source-page locations, file sizes and SHA-256 checksums. Original source-file hashes are distinguished from hashes of extracted pages and rendered crops. Negative inspected-page results and blocked leads are preserved as such.

Only Thompson is a strong newly recovered independent organizational mark in this pass. Shared-use specimens, a website masthead, name-only records and unresolved scans remain separately labelled so the next batch can be reviewed without inflating the accepted count.
