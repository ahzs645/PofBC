# Provincial Emergency Program lettering and emblem

The July 1993 *Joint Emergency Preparedness Program Guidelines* has two organizational signatures on its cover. The upright JEPP initials identify the funding initiative. At the foot of the page, the provincial PEP monogram accompanies a lighter three-line name. A B.C. Environment receipt stamp dates a provincial compliments slip with heavier lettering to October 16, 1997. [Compliments slip, PDF page 50](https://docs.openinfo.gov.bc.ca/d37393013a_response_package_fnr-2013-00153.pdf#page=50)

The 2003 *Risk Management for Outdoor Programs* guide reproduces the heavier lettering in the supplied black and blue examples. Neither publication identifies the original typeface, designer or adoption date. [1993 guidelines, PDF page 2](https://www.publicsafety.gc.ca/lbrr/archives/ua%20929.c2%20b75%201993-eng.pdf#page=2) · [2003 guide, PDF pages 1–2](https://www.nic.bc.ca/_resources/pdf/adventure-guiding-risk-guide.pdf#page=1)

The editable SVG contains an ellipse with a triangular opening, 23 horizontal bands, separate slanted P–E–P initials, connecting bands, and a drawn registration symbol. Each of the 26 letters in the three-line name has its own outline. The black and dark-blue versions use the same geometry. Their screen ink values follow the supplied examples and carry no claim about an official spot colour.

## Type comparison

The 2003 cover embeds the 518 × 155 pixel logo as a single image. The document’s text-font list describes the surrounding page. It cannot identify the lettering baked into the logo. For each candidate below, the comparison adjusted the additional slant, fitted each letter to its observed bounds, and measured mean intersection-over-union across the 26 letter masks. The single-storey *g*, double-storey *a*, shape of the *r* and angle of the vertical strokes matter as much as overall line width.

The reconstruction uses Nimbus Sans Narrow Bold Oblique with an additional horizontal shear of 0.245 relative to glyph height, individual advances and fitted proportions. The first two candidates differ by only 0.3 percentage points. The source raster cannot establish the historical typeface.

At the 2003 image’s native scale the final complete drawing has about 89% foreground overlap, including 88% for the assembled wordmark. These figures compare against the published 2003 image. The supplied colour samples were compared visually. The 1993 scan independently documents the earlier, lighter lettering.
