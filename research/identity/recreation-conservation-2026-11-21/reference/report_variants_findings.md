# Documented annual-report variants, 1970–1975/76

## Main finding

The five-mark rounded-square system is documented in the **1971, 1972 and 1973 report editions**. The 1972 report still uses the original deer-and-fish Fish and Wildlife square. The 1973 interior dividers replace it with the narrow oval **F&W** emblem plus a separate name panel. The 1973 back cover nevertheless retains the older deer-and-fish mark. This proves coexistence within one publication; it does not establish a single universal replacement date.

The 1974 and 1975/76 reports use another layout: type, photographs, horizontal rules and branch-opening pages on tinted or dark fields. Their chapter openings no longer use the rounded-square family. This is a change in these publications, not evidence that every mark ceased being used elsewhere.

## Types of variation actually established

1. **Five original symbols.** The four-lobe department mark, deer-and-fish square, paired-spiral Parks mark, BCPM monogram, and three-fish/grid mark.
2. **Five divider applications.** The department mark stays dark at lower left. The branch symbols form a right-hand column. The selected branch is printed dark and is identified by a nearby caption; the others appear pale. The General Administration leaf has all four branch symbols pale. These are layout/tint variants of the same base artwork.
3. **Front-cover departmental lockups.** The small four-lobe mark sits above a two-line department name and annual-report heading. The provincial coat of arms appears separately above it. Covers in 1971 and 1972 reverse the lettering and mark to pale on dark green. The 1973 cover uses dark lettering and mark on cream.
4. **Vertical family stack.** Four small branch marks sit above an enlarged department mark. The 1971 and 1972 back covers print this pale on dark green. The 1973 back cover prints it dark on cream. Reversal changes the treatment, not the logo geometry.
5. **Fish and Wildlife artwork change.** The old square is still on the 1972 divider; the two-panel oval F&W/name lockup appears on the 1973 interior dividers. Both generations appear in the 1973 report because the back cover retains the old square.
6. **Fisheries name change.** The three-fish symbol is retained beside **Marine Resources Branch [Commercial Fisheries]** in 1973. This is a name/lockup change rather than a new fish design. The introduction, printed p9 / PDF10, explains the broader remit behind the new name.
7. **Separate Parks emblem.** A shield with the province silhouette and dogwood, labelled PARKS / BRITISH COLUMBIA, appears on the 1973 Parks organization chart, printed AA79 / PDF80. The paired-spiral symbol remains on the Parks divider, AA77 / PDF78. The two coexist. The later proposal sentence at AA105 / PDF106 does not identify its drawing or designer, so the chart emblem cannot automatically be equated with that proposal.
8. **Earlier department emblem.** The 1970 report has an oval dogwood department badge on thin rectangular dividers with serif headings. General Administration is PDF10; equivalent chapter dividers are PDFs14,40,68,90. That is an earlier design found in the checked reports, not proof of its first use.

## Later-report boundary checks

The complete 1970, 1972, 1974 and 1975/76 official reports were surveyed as page contact sheets, followed by readable rendering of the relevant artwork. The 1971 and 1973 colour reports were examined at the named divider and cover pages.

The 1974 branch-opening pages are PDFs14,68,94,124 (printed positions DD13,DD67,DD93,DD123). The 1975/76 equivalents are PDFs14,70,100,128,160,168 (H13,H69,H99,H127,H159,H167); the latter two sections are Outdoor Recreation and Information & Education. Their opening pages provide no additional matching square icons. The 1976/77 report was also downloaded and its early pages inspected; it continues plain typography, but it was not exhaustively searched for every incidental mark.

No source in this report survey establishes a designer or a comprehensive identity manual. “All variants” should therefore be presented as **the documented variants located**, not every historical application ever issued.
