# StrongerBC artwork review

The March 2024 graphic standards document four layouts, each in full colour positive, full colour reverse, solid black and solid white. The gallery already held the eight public-kit SVGs for the plain name and an earlier, editable “for everyone” endorsed drawing with a flat sun and grey divider. The review recovered eight 2024 tagline specimens and added four original CMYK print PDFs for the plain layouts. The earlier drawing keeps its own entry and date context. ## Where the CleanBC template fits

| Shared cataloguing decision | StrongerBC application |
| --- | --- |
| Group by design, then select an evidenced source colourway | A light card uses its positive artwork. A dark card uses the published reverse. Choosing black or white explicitly preserves the selected file and puts it on a legible ground. Different drawings can belong to the same selector. |
| Preserve a source locator, file digest and acquisition method | A and B point to archive members. C and D point to a guide page, specimen bounds and SVG checksum. A dated sample documents appearance at that date. |
| Attach a print export to the corresponding version | The RGB-positive and RGB-reverse plain marks each offer their same-layout CMYK PDF. These are production files for the same two designs. |
| Keep usage rules and relationships with the family | The design choice here depends on who is communicating and whether the tagline is present. CleanBC’s program descriptors, partner and support acknowledgements, palette, lowercase-c spacing unit and tiny alternative belong to CleanBC. |

Both galleries use the same source-version helper and preview pipeline. The StrongerBC plain standalone RGB and solid pairs keep their existing, geometry-verified palette editor and legacy links. Text for headlines and body copy is a separate template opportunity. The general guide specifies Noto Sans for print/static digital materials, Noto Sans SemiCondensed for social graphics, and BC Sans for government web pages. Infrastructure signs have their own standardized copy and layouts. These specifications supply no basis for typesetting replacement logo lettering. [Shared helper](../../../src/gallery/programMarkVersions.js) · [General brand information, pp. 8–10](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/services-policies-for-government/policies-procedures-standards/web-content-development-guides/corporate-identity-assets/visid-pdfs/strongerbc_generalbrandinfo_v3.pdf#page=8)

The endorsed RGB kit shows why the selector needs a real file for each treatment. In the positive SVG, the lettering and landscape use the same navy. In the reverse, the lettering turns white while the landscape changes to blue-grey (#7B8CAC) and “BC” stays gold. The public guide reproduces print colours. Its converted gold and navy differ from the RGB kit’s #E3A82B and #234075. The gallery preserves each source’s inks. Page 8 of the general guide also reproduces the four positive arrangements as raster samples. ## How the brands connect

The recovery plan launched in September 2020. The broader economic plan followed in February 2022. The May 2022 matrix includes CleanBC, WorkBC, ChildCareBC and Buy BC in the economic-plan story and assigns each a specific communications role. Better Homes and Go Electric illustrate CleanBC-specific messages. Climate investments discussed in the context of jobs or the whole economic plan can carry StrongerBC. The same distinction applies to WorkBC services versus province-wide employment investment, child care services versus their economic benefits, and local food promotion versus agricultural investment. These are communications relationships. The matrix supplies no evidence for a new joint logo or a change of agency ownership. ## Provenance and outstanding evidence

- All eight existing SVGs matched their public ZIP members byte for byte. The four print PDFs were copied unchanged. Each PDF contains vector drawing commands with no raster image objects. The endorsed archive members are under `BCID-StrongerBC_EndorsedMark/`, the standalone files under `StrongerBC_Wordmark/`. [Endorsed kit](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/services-policies-for-government/policies-procedures-standards/web-content-development-guides/corporate-identity-assets/visid-files/bcid-strongerbc_endorsedmark.zip) · [Wordmark kit](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/services-policies-for-government/policies-procedures-standards/web-content-development-guides/corporate-identity-assets/visid-files/strongerbc_wordmark.zip)
- The eight tagline SVGs isolate the printed paths from the public PDF. PyMuPDF removed the surrounding frame, test ground and material outside the registered rectangle, then exported the remaining artwork as SVG. Bounds use PDF points from the top-left page corner. None of these SVGs embeds a bitmap or requires a font. [Extraction register](../../../src/gallery/strongerBCSources.json)
- The Province lists the original “for everyone” production packages as restricted to IDIR. The public specimen extractions record what the guide actually shows. Contents of the restricted packages remain unverified. The project’s earlier January 2021 guide URL returned 404, leaving the complete set of early treatments open. The 2024 guide explicitly shows plain and tagline layouts concurrently.
