# ICBC logo history — expanded research notes

Edition 02 | Research checked 27 September 2026

## Reading the timeline

The board shows five **evidence groups**, not five proven, mutually exclusive lifespans. An official filing, a first-use claim and a dated publication answer different questions. The data therefore keeps `adoption_date` and `retirement_date` null where the exact events remain unverified. Do not derive historical validity ranges merely by joining adjacent cards. The “2008 → 2026” label connects known evidence; it is not a day-by-day audit.

## 1974: a previously missing early emblem

CIPO 0374886 records a use claim from **1 March 1974** and filing on **6 May 1974**. The source drawing is supplied in `source-assets/`. Its upper component is retained in the new standalone vector. **The vector is approximate, unhatched construction linework**, not a recovered colour master. The record says the drawing is lined for colour without claiming colour as a feature. The June 2023 cancellation concerns the registration, not the logo’s last public use. [S10]

No verified 1973 example was located. Establishment of the corporation in 1973 does not independently date this artwork. [S1]

## 1989: a related arrangement, not a proven launch

Official-mark record 0903609 documents filing on **12 January 1989** and public notice on **17 May 1989**. It depicts the half-disc beside the abbreviated name. This does not date the separate full-corporate-name arrangement in the supplied reference. Both reconstructed versions are retained. Their relationship to the 1974 arrangement, including possible overlap, remains unresolved. [S2; U1]

## Circa 1992: the serif road badge

The **1992 annual report back cover** shows the narrow serif road badge. The visible print code is **PI 151 (2 93)**, consistent with February 1993 printing; that interpretation is distinguished from the report year. [S11]

CIPO 1326195 separately claims insurance-service use from **March 1992**, while its filing dates to 2006. Taken together, these sources justify a qualified **circa-1992 road-family date**, not an exact launch for every letterform. [S3; S11]

The existing serif vector was originally rebuilt from the supplied 1994-labelled photograph. It is retained as an approximation. Discovering an earlier documentary example does not make those paths exact or certify the original font. [U2]

## 2000s: the square sans-serif badge

The square-corner, heavier sans-serif badge appears on the **January 2007** and **January 2008** service-plan covers. [S12; S13] The provided vector’s folder is labelled 2005, but that label remains separate from independent dating. Its path geometry is preserved. [U3]

The exact serif-to-sans changeover has not been established. In particular, “seen in 2007 and 2008” does not mean “used only from 2007 to 2008”, and the evidence does not establish that every application changed at once.

## 2008 onward: rounded badge and overlap

The rounded-corner registry record was filed on **21 January 2008** and explicitly describes three rounded corners. Its older service-use claims should not be treated as proof that every detail of the rounded drawing existed in 1992. [S4]

The blue colour record, filed in 2012, claims use from **January 2008**. Thus 2012 is not treated as its launch year. [S5] The January 2008 plan still has the square version, whereas January 2009 shows the rounded version. A rollout around 2008 is an **inference** from these overlapping observations, not a documented single-day switch. [S13; S14; S5]

The blue rounded badge also appears on the official **August 2026** annual service plan report. [S6]

## Brand system, authorship and colour variants

Stu Ross’s portfolio credits a Brand Owner’s Manual, web branding bulletin, police-partnership brochure and Driver Risk advertisements, with Wasserman+Partners identified as client. That is evidence of communications/identity-system work, not proof that he originated the ICBC logo. A dependable project year is not established by this page. [S15]

The previous research identified a PS&Co case study discussing purple, lozenge labels, signage and digital applications around the road badge. The URL now returns an anti-bot interstitial during the package recheck, so it is retained as a **research lead**, not a freshly verified launch announcement. No new logo era or precise start date is assigned. [S16]

The supplied orange artwork has the same modern paths as the blue/black derivatives. It remains a **colour variant**; permanent replacement of blue and a specific campaign purpose are unverified. The supplied orange fill is preserved. The blue fill is an explicitly declared recolour sampled from the original supplied blue reference, not a certified colour specification. [U4; U5]

## Remaining gaps

Early adoption and retirement dates; whether related lockups coexisted; a verified 1973 example; original colours, designers and typefaces; the serif-to-sans transition year; a single confirmed modern rollout date; the orange treatment’s purpose and duration; and reliable dates for the broader brand-system projects.
