<p class=MsoNormal><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:11.0pt'>&nbsp;</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-left:.5in'><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-left:.5in'><u><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:11.0pt'>886 Wing - Relocation of 419

<p class=MsoNormal><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:11.0pt'>&nbsp;</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-left:.5in'><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:
11.0pt'>Mr. Daws outlined the role of the Moose Squadron RCAF in World War II
and noted the Squadron was disbanded in 1995 at which time, as a lasting
memento to the City that adopted them, a CFI Freedom Fighter airplane was
presented to the City and was mounted at  Fulton Field.  He indicated Lt. Col.
John Laidler, Commanding Officer for 419 Squadron, and Mayor Cliff Branchflower
participated at the dedication of the CFI Freedom Fighter and the official
&quot;standing down&quot; of the Squadron and the return of the freedom of the
city to the City of Kamloops before a large contingent of service personnel
from Cold Lake and members of 886 (Overlander) RCAF Wing, AFAC.  He indicated
he was quite pleased to hear that the Moose Squadron was to be made active once
more, but was displeased the City had been lax in its custody of the invaluable
419 Squadron colours and other artifacts, including 'Bruce the Moose'.  Mr.
Daws indicated the 886 (Overlander) RCAF Wing sought assurance that before the
occasion in July when the 419 Squadron will receive back these items of such
great importance that the situation would be rectified and the image and
glorious traditions of the RCAF would be made safe.</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:11.0pt'>&nbsp;</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-left:.5in'><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:
11.0pt'>Mayor Rothenburger thanked Mr. Daws for his presentation and assured
him that the paraphernalia related to the 419 (Moose) Squadron RCAF would be
well taken care of until such time as it was retrieved by the Squadron in July
2000.</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-left:1.5in'><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:

in its entirety and re-enact as follows:</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:11.0pt'>&nbsp;</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-left:2.0in;text-indent:-.5in'><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:11.0pt'>1200    <u>Approval</u></span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:11.0pt'>&nbsp;</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-left:2.0in'><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:
11.0pt'>A permit approved by the Assistant Administrator/City Engineer for the
right to provide, install or place chairs, tables, benches or any other objects
within the road right-of-way for the purpose of extending a business outdoors
must first be obtained prior to the placement of said objects.</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:11.0pt'>&nbsp;</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-left:2.0in;text-indent:-.5in'><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:11.0pt'>1201    <u>Requirements for Permit</u></span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:11.0pt'>&nbsp;</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-left:2.0in'><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:
11.0pt'>The permit application required for placement of any objects cited in
Section 1200 must be completed and submitted for approval along with a site
plan, clearly indicating the extent and location of proposed work, construction
details and any other information deemed relevant by the Assistant
Administrator/City Engineer.</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:11.0pt'>&nbsp;</span></p>

<p class=MsoNormal style='margin-left:2.0in'><span lang=EN-GB style='font-size:
11.0pt'>Tables and chairs to be used by customers for food or beverage
consumption.  The plans must indicate a barricade enclosed on all three sides
with an entrance on the street side of the barricade.  The plan must also
provide the height of the barricade to be no less than 0.75 m and no greater
than 1.0 m.  No barricade is to be attached to the sidewalk or have any sharp
or pointed pickets that could cause injury.</span></p>
