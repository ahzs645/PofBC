# Evidence and remaining uncertainties

## S1 — contemporary change announcement

**BC Ferry Services Inc. / British Columbia Ministry of Transportation. “New BC Ferry Company to Improve Services.” 2 April 2003.**

https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/archive/2001-2005/2003tran0017-000313.htm

This is the strongest chronology source: a contemporary official statement that retires the dogwood, introduces the new wave and describes the age of the outgoing logo. It supports the broad dogwood era and the exact 2003 change announcement. Its approximate age statement supports the *inference* c.1978; it does not supply an actual 1978 launch document or identify every historical drawing.

The announced phase-in over the following year is a rollout plan, not evidence that the whole fleet was repainted immediately or that completion occurred on a particular date. Do not introduce a separate “2004 logo” merely because an individual vessel acquired the new livery then.

## S2 — original 1963 brochure, hosted by ExploreNorth

**British Columbia Toll Authority Ferry System. 1963 season brochure.**

Collector's page identifying the issue:
https://explorenorth.com/bc/bc_ferries-1963.html

Cover scan inspected:
https://explorenorth.com/bc/images/bc_ferries-1963a.jpg

The cover contains an illustrated green dogwood flag with softer, black-outlined white lobes and a yellow centre. It supplies a dated visual example that was absent from the user's archive. The interpretation in this package normalizes the drawing and simplifies the flag; the original scanned print should be consulted for exact illustration details.

The large serif “B.C. FERRIES” cover headline has not been promoted to a separate corporate-logo era. Brochure typography alone is not a sufficient basis for doing so. The scan is linked, not bundled; the downloaded ZIP contains only your supplied original references and the newly created assets.

## S3 — current identity check

**BC Ferries. Investor Overview — September 2026. Cover, PDF page 1.**

https://www.bcferries.com/web_image/h7f/h2d/9106869944350.pdf

The cover visibly uses the wave-and-wordmark identity. This independently checks current use through September 2026. It does not prove that no tiny production-level contour changes occurred over the entire period since 2003.

## Unresolved research questions

1. An original late-1970s identity manual, dated launch notice or consecutive annual-report covers could turn the approximate c.1978 date into a documented transition.
2. Dated print examples are needed to establish when the specific geometric flower and uppercase wordmark appeared, and whether they changed together.
3. The earlier outlined flower may have multiple print and textile drawings; one 1963 illustration cannot define all of them.
4. The design studios/individual designers, official historical colour specifications, and patch production dates remain unverified.

S1 identifies the Maritime Museum of British Columbia as custodian of the ferry heritage material at the changeover. Its relevant holdings are a documented archival lead, but no unexamined catalogue item is cited here as proof.
