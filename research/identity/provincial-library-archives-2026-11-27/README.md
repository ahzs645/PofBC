# Provincial Library & Archives emblem - vector reconstruction

Prepared 27 November 2026 from the supplied 1116 x 1410 RGBA reference.

## Files

- `provincial-library-archives.svg`: editable vector master. The original coordinate system and proportions are retained. Exterior openings are transparent; the emblem has white interior fills and black engraving.
- `provincial-library-archives.pdf`: A4 print version containing vector paths. Its artwork is 186 mm wide on the page.
- `provincial-library-archives-transparent.png`: 4000-pixel-high transparent raster export for convenience.
- `provincial-library-archives-preview.png`: preview on white.
- `provincial-library-archives-comparison.png`: aligned source, vector, overlay, and enlarged animal details.
- `reference/source-reference.png`: unchanged supplied image, kept separately from the vector artwork.
- `scripts/`: reconstruction code and the manually defined crown backing paths.
- `verification.json`: artifact properties and targeted shading checks.

## Reconstruction decisions

The main method is source-derived cubic Bezier contour reconstruction with selective cleanup, followed by visual comparison and local correction. The original ornamental lettering is retained as outlines. A substitute typeface has not been introduced.

The first full comparison revealed excessive darkness in the shield's densely engraved flag fields and wave bands. Those regions received a separate tonal threshold while the structural outlines, rays, vertical flag hatching, and facial details retained their own settings. Tiny printing pits were cleaned more strongly in the lettering than in the engraved fields.

The source cutout contains patchy partial transparency inside the crown caps and a small central highlight. Three white backing paths restore these enclosed interior surfaces while preserving actual gaps outside the crown. They are on a separate layer and can be hidden if exact source transparency is desired.

The vector keeps the asymmetrical scroll folds, separate stag antlers, unequal ram horns, lion face, crowns, sun rays, and complete motto `SPLENDOR SINE OCCASU.`.

## Editing

Open the SVG in a vector editor. All visible artwork is made of editable paths, and the lettering requires no fonts. Named layers separate the white support, connected framework, independent detail contours, original lettering, and the crown restoration. Within the lettering layer, each word has its own group. Some original engraving is physically connected and remains a compound path; the animal silhouettes are not all detached objects.

The supplied image is the fidelity limit for small details. This reconstruction preserves its engraved character and its slight hand-drawn irregularities.

## Verification

The SVG was inspected for image elements, live text, filters, and duplicate IDs. It contains no raster image or font dependencies. The PDF was inspected and rendered: one A4 page, no embedded raster images, and no font objects. Enlarged comparisons checked lettering, scrolls, lion face, both supporters, antlers, horns, shield hatching, and the motto. A contrasting background check verified the white fills and exterior transparency.

## Reproducing the master

The included Python generator requires Pillow, NumPy, SciPy, lxml, and native libpotrace. The recorded fitting engine was libpotrace 1.16. Run from the extracted package:

```sh
python scripts/rebuild_emblem.py
```

This creates a new `output/` directory with the reconstructed SVG, plus QA masks under `work/`. The final default parameters are stored in the generator and SVG metadata. Inkscape was used to render the exports. The PDF wrapper is generated by `scripts/prepare_delivery.py` after rebuilding.

## When the emblem was used (search, 27 November 2026)

No dated sighting of this emblem was found. What was checked:

- **Legislative Library Digital Collections** (DSpace REST search for items by the Provincial Library,
  the Provincial Library and Archives and the Provincial Archives: 182 items). First pages were read of
  the Archives Indexer's report for 1933, the Cataloguing Department's report for 1940, the annual
  reports for 1946, 1950 and 1957, and "Some books and documents in the Provincial Library and Archives
  … on the Columbia River Basin" (1944). Two carry an arms device, and neither is this emblem:
  - the [Cataloguing Department report for 1940](https://digitalcollections.llbc.leg.bc.ca/handle/123456789/87080),
    dated 12 January 1941, is on "PROVINCIAL LIBRARY AND ARCHIVES, VICTORIA, B.C." letterhead under the
    government's device ("The Government of the Province of British Columbia" under the arms);
  - the [1944 list](https://digitalcollections.llbc.leg.bc.ca/handle/123456789/338996), dated 24 August
    1944, uses the same device over "PROVINCIAL LIBRARY, VICTORIA, B.C."
  So by 1941 the joint body's letterhead used the government device, not this emblem.
- **Internet Archive.** The 1902 "Rules and regulations governing travelling libraries"
  ([rulesregulations00brituoft](https://archive.org/details/rulesregulations00brituoft)), catalogued
  under "Provincial Library and Archives, Victoria", has the same government device on its cover, with
  the pre-1906 arms. The 1898 travelling-library finding list has no device.
- **UBC Rare Books and Special Collections bookplates.** A lead, not a sighting:
  [[Bookplate for British Columbia Library and Archives]](https://open.library.ubc.ca/collections/bookplate/items/1.0215134)
  (BP GEN CAN I P768a): "Black ink on white paper. All text in scrolls above and below coat of arms of
  British Columbia", 6.3 × 9.5 cm, language Latin, date "[unknown]". The layout fits this emblem: names
  on scrolls above the arms, the motto on the scroll below. The image could not be retrieved (the page
  loads it by script), so the match is not confirmed. If it matches, the emblem is a bookplate, and the
  supplied reference may come from one.

Not reached: the Archives' Memoir series (1914 on) and the reports in UBC's BC Sessional Papers, whose
page images load only by script.
