# ASDT and the Administrative Services Collaborative, 27 November 2026

Research behind two Initiatives entries: `asdt` (Administrative Service Delivery Transformation) and,
beneath it as the programme's successor, `administrative-services-collaborative`. Their data is
`src/initiatives/asdt.js`; their index entries are in `src/initiatives/initiatives.js`. Tests:
`src/initiatives/asdt.test.js`.

## What they were

**ASDT** was a joint programme of the ministry responsible for post-secondary education, the 25 public
universities, colleges and institutes, and BCNET. It set out to share non-academic administration:
joint buying, finance, IT and enterprise systems.

- Budget 2012 targeted savings in post-secondary non-academic administration: $20M in 2013/14 and a
  further $30M in 2014/15. The "Opportunity Assessment Phase" began on 25 June 2012, and its terms of
  reference were approved on 28 June 2012 ([project page, Wayback 11 Feb 2013][page]). The ministry
  was then Advanced Education, Innovation and Technology.
- Deloitte's *BC Post-Secondary Administrative Service Delivery Transformation Opportunity Assessment
  Final Report* is dated 12 February 2013 ([report][deloitte], cover). It sorts opportunities into
  three tiers (p. 8). The ministry posted it on 22 February 2013 and took tiers 1 and 2 forward. The
  "Implementation Phase" began in March 2013 ([key messages][keys]).
- The 2013/14 Progress Report ([August 2014][pr1314], p. 6) gives:
  - July 2012: launch;
  - June 2013: the first sector partnership forum, with more than 60 people from all 25 institutions;
  - October 2013: all 25 institutions became BCNET members, and the ASDT Collaboration Office was
    set up;
  - May 2014: the Joint Procurement Consortium launched with all 25 as members.

  Savings for 2013/14 were $10.51M (p. 14). An executive committee chaired by the deputy minister
  oversaw the work, and a steering committee ran it (pp. 3–4).
- Releases of [19 January 2015][rel1] and [22 December 2015][rel2]: $10.5M in 2013/14 and $30.66M in
  2014/15, against a goal of $50M over three years.
- The 2015/16 Progress Report ([PDF made 19 December 2016][pr1516]):
  - procurement work continued "through a two-year pilot with BCNET, effective April 1, 2016"
    (BCNETSource, p. 10);
  - a Finance Centre of Expertise was launched in 2016/17 (p. 37);
  - $36.64M in benefits for 2015/16, $30.03M of them BCNET-led (p. 40);
  - governance on pp. 52–57.

  In its p. 40 table, "TLAS – ASC" is the *Administrative Systems Consortium*, a Colleague ERP group
  of nine institutions. It is not the later Administrative Services Collaborative.
- The ministry's annual service plan reports: [2014/15][asr1415] p. 23 (consortium, May 2014);
  [2015/16][asr1516] p. 26 (31 projects; a three-year strategic plan); [2016/17][asr1617] p. 29
  ($77.9M "since its inception in 2013/14"). The 2017/18 and 2018/19 reports do not mention ASDT.
- [BCNET Annual Review 2017|2018][bcnet] p. 18: "in 2016, we launched BCNETSource in partnership
  with the … (ASDT) Initiative."

**The Administrative Services Collaborative (ASC)** is the same programme renamed.

- The *ASC Strategic Plan 2019/20 – 2021/22* ([plan][plan2019]) is dated "MAY 2019" on p. 11, though
  the PDF was made on 28 October 2020. On p. 3 the co-chairs say "we are adopting a new name: the
  Administrative Services Collaborative". On p. 4 it says "Founded in 2012/13 as the Administrative
  Service Delivery Transformation (ASDT)".
- The [infographic][info2020], "updated November 2020", says on p. 2: "In 2020, the Administrative
  Services Collaborative was launched with a new name and updated strategic plan."
- The ministry's ASC page was first captured on [6 August 2020][page2020], with the footnote "Formerly
  known as the Administrative Service Delivery Transformation (ASDT) initiative". The [live page][ascpage]
  was updated on 27 April 2026. It lists the partners as the 25 institutions, BCNET and the ministry.
- A [2022/23 – 2025/26 plan][plan2023] followed, launched in early 2023. The [progress report][pr2026]
  (PDF made 13 April 2026, p. 2) describes a leadership group of senior people from the institutions,
  BCNET and the ministry.

**How it relates to BCNET.** BCNET is a partner, not the host: the ASC's page, plans and contact
address are the ministry's. BCNET carried the shared procurement (BCNETSource) and IT contracts that
produced most of the reported benefits. No ASC page was found on bc.net; that absence is not proof.

**Dates.** No source gives an exact day for ASDT's start or for the rename.

- Start: the sources give 25 June 2012 (the assessment), July 2012 (launch), "2012/13" (founding) and
  "inception in 2013/14" (benefits). The site dates the assessment by its day and the launch by its
  month.
- Rename: May 2019 (the plan) and 2020 (the launch).
- The bulletin's "four years ago at … the Vancouver Airport" meeting is not dated anywhere.

What it says:

- **p. 1.** The Sector Executive Lead was a two-year pilot funded from the start of 2015/16. Barry
  Coulson held it on secondment from Langara College, and it went on hold from 1 April 2017. The
  bulletin speaks for "the partnership between the sector and the Ministry of Advanced Education".
- **p. 2.** Dennis Silva (BCNET Chief Procurement Officer) says the procurement pilot at BCNET began
  almost a year earlier, and that he and Coulson started on 1 April 2015. Coulson says ASDT was
  launched "four years ago" at a sector meeting at Vancouver Airport, to act on the Deloitte report,
  and that almost $78M in benefits had been reported.
- **pp. 3–4.** The Partnership Forum met at SFU Harbour Centre on 1 December 2016, with about 80
  people. It gives $36.66M for 2015/16; the progress report gives $36.64M. It also covers the Finance
  CoE and ERP. The co-chairs were Peter Smailes and Jeanne Sedun. The distribution list follows.
- **p. 5.** The Digital Learning Resources Network: seed funding first sought in February 2016, and
  extended to 31 March 2017 at the 30 November 2016 meeting.
- **p. 6.** BCNETSource agreements.

The masthead column shows the logos of the ministry, BCNET and the 25 institutions, plus one
unidentified red and gold crest beside Selkirk College. That is the bulletin's design, not a
membership list.

**ASDT vector extraction.** On p. 58 the five circles are native PDF radial shadings (`/Sh0 sh`, five
times, each clipped to a circle). MuPDF's SVG rasterises shadings, so its extraction kept only the
lines and letters. Poppler's `pdftocairo -svg` (26.07.0) writes them as five `<radialGradient>`s.
`extract_cairo_region.py` runs the same region filter over cairo's page SVG. Cairo places glyphs with
`x`/`y` on `<use>`, so the script rewrites each as the identical `translate()` first.

- Region: `[240, 640, 480, 725]`, PDF points from the top-left.
- Result: 9 paths (the lines and the gradient-filled circles under their clip paths) and 60 glyph
  outlines. The "asdt" glyphs are from the report's embedded Century subset; the tagline's are Corbel.
- No images and no live text. Re-running the script reproduces the file byte for byte (SHA-256
  `a8683f0b…6cdd7`).

Renders checked in Chrome: `asdt-vector-check.png` and `asc-vector-check.png`. On the Initiatives
pages the artwork panel stays light in dark mode, so the source colourways are legible in both
themes. No gallery display copy was made, because the gallery's collected data was being edited by
another task.

**Other sightings, not extracted:**
