# Forest Genetics Council and related forestry identities

The white horizontal signature in the supplied example survives in the Council’s own website header. It has 41 outlined paths and seven tapered tree polygons. The dark-green header version uses the same arrangement. The ministry’s website inventory and a photographic backdrop are unnecessary to render either SVG. The Council’s stylesheet also links a separate stacked white SVG. All three web assets retain the publisher’s geometry. [Council website](https://forestgeneticsbc.ca/) · [Stacked SVG](https://forestgeneticsbc.ca/wp-content/themes/enfold-child/img/fgc-logo-stacked-white.svg)

The spring 1999 newsletter records that the Chief Forester brought the coastal and interior councils together in March 1997 and endorsed the permanent council’s name and strategic plans in July 1998. Its masthead has the FGC initials and seven trees in a black panel. The early report series documents the white-on-blue arrangement by 2001/02. These publications establish dated sightings. Formal adoption and design credit remain open. [TICtalk, spring 1999, p. 1](https://forestgeneticsbc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/TICtalk-1999.pdf) · [2001/02 annual report, cover](https://forestgeneticsbc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/FGCAR_2001_02_Abbr.pdf)

## Why the catalogue missed the council

At revision `fcc8434`, PofBC’s October acquisition inventory surveyed 183 current and former Crown corporations and health organizations. Subsequent passes added selected councils and other public institutions. FGC was mentioned in a research lead concerning Forest Renewal BC. It had no registered identity, collected file or owning council record. The original four-group inventory explains why FGC would not have surfaced in that pass. There is no evidence of an editorial decision to exclude it.
