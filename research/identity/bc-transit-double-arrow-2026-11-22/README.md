# BC Transit's double arrow beside its name, 22 November 2026

The red double arrow beside "BC Transit" in blue was on the roof of the red-and-white Gallery In
Transit paper model, but had no entry of its own in Logos. The paper models came in through their
own pipeline, and the BC Transit collection went straight from the Urban Transit Authority to the
flag. This adds it as the collection's second era, `bct-double-arrow`, with one entry,
`bct-double-arrow-paper-model`.

## The artwork

`artwork/bc-transit/bc-transit-double-arrow-paper-model.svg`, registered in
`scripts/build-crown-marks.mjs`. It is cut from the model's printable PDF,
`public/paper-models/gallery-in-transit/bc-transit-foldable.packcad.pdf`:

1. The page was written as SVG with `pdftocairo -svg`.
2. Only the drawn elements inside the region (205, 305)–(395, 375) pt were kept.

That leaves three paths: the two arrow halves and the outlined lettering. No images, text or
references remain, and the geometry is untouched. The fills were rewritten from `rgb()`
percentages as the same colours in hex, #f31410 and #172bb2. Those are the model file's values,
not brand specifications.

It is classed as a reconstruction. The model is a modern drawing of the 1985 poster bus, so it
cannot vouch for the original letterforms, spacing or inks.

## Evidence

- **Henri Robideau, "Gallery In Transit Vancouver"**
  (https://henrirobideau.com/gallery-in-transit-vancouver.html). The exhibition ran 8–27 June
  1985, and the poster was "printed on card stock as a pop-out fold-up model BC Transit bus". It
  dates a use of this arrangement, not its introduction or retirement.
- **BC Transit, "Our history"** (https://www.bctransit.com/about/history/). The Urban Transit
  Authority formed in 1979 and was renamed BC Transit in 1982. This arrangement is no older than
  the name.
- **Urban Transit Authority, Graphic Standards Manual** (TRID record 165550,
  https://trid.trb.org/View/165550). A catalogue record of the authority's graphic standards,
  covering the mark, colours, type and vehicles. No copy has been seen, and the record gives no
  publication date. It is the lead for the mark's original construction.
- **TransLink, The Buzzer, "75 facts about Vancouver's trolleybus system"** (16 August 2023,
  https://buzzer.translink.ca/2023/08/75-facts-about-trolleybus/). Fact 8: the trolleybuses were
  painted in the UTA's orange and white on 2 April 1982, then "in August" the Province renamed the
  UTA BC Transit, and a red, white and blue scheme followed. The year beside "August" is implied
  by the April 1982 date, not stated.
- **Museum of Vancouver, H2015.37.361a-c, "Pamphlet, BC Transit, handyDART, c. 1980s"**
  (https://openmovportal.ca/argus/final/Portal/Main.aspx?component=AAFG&record=b565fe34-98af-435c-85de-9195188cbb78;
  accession 2015.9.361). Dated 1982–1985, estimated from the adoption of the name BC Transit in
  1982 and from the pamphlets predating BC Transit's change of logo to the BC flag in 1985. Colours
  red, white, blue and black. Checked 22 November 2026: the record's text does not describe the
  arrow, so the "white arrow on red" reported to the project rests on its photographs, which were
  not examined here. Its range is the museum's estimate for these objects, not a retirement date
  for the mark.
