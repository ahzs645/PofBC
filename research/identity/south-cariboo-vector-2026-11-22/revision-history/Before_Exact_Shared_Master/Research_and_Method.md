# South Cariboo Community Health Council
## Vector reconstruction and historical research

Prepared 22 November 2026. This is a newly constructed historical reconstruction, not an original vector recovered from the council.

## What was established

- The colour reference supplied for this task is the native 315 by 141 pixel logo image from the council's Health Plan. The decoded attachment and the PDF image match after applying the PDF placement's vertical flip.
- The plan's cover gives an approval date of 1 November 1999. The surviving PDF was created in May 2000 and archived in September 2000. These dates identify the evidence; they do not establish when the logo was designed or first adopted.
- The 19-page PDF contains one JPEG logo and no drawing paths. There is no hidden vector master to extract from this file.
- A separate 239 by 258 pixel black tree emblem survives from the original council website. Its solid silhouettes differ from the textured colour lockup; it was used only as corroborating evidence for the two-tree composition.
- A 200 by 100 pixel black-and-gray lockup is preserved from a 2004 archive capture of a thank-you image. Related 2000 and 2001 HTML links the same image filename, but the recovered image payload was verified only from 2004. It is evidence of a genuine monochrome application, not a securely dated 2000 image payload.
- No original vector file, original colour specification, adoption record, or substantiated designer credit was found in the examined material.

## Organization and dates

| Date | Evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 14 December 1994 | M431/1994, signed 13 December, explicitly effective 14 December | Designated as 100 Mile House & District Community Health Council. |
| 23 May 1997 | Date signed on M225/1997 | Order replaces the old name with South Cariboo Community Health Council. A separate effective/deposit date is not established here. |
| 1 November 1999 | Approval date printed on the Health Plan cover | The green logo belongs to this dated document. Its surviving PDF has May 2000 creation metadata. |
| 30 March 2000 | Legislative debate, printed p.14684 | Chair Alan Boyd is commended following the health-centre opening. |
| 12 December 2001 | B.C. Reg.293/2001, deposited on this date | The council is a named constituent in the amalgamation into Interior Health Authority. |

The Health Plan (printed p.3 / PDF p.4) describes an estimated catchment population of 16,000 in 1997, including 100 Mile House, 108 Mile Ranch, and surrounding lake and ranch country across approximately 5,000 square kilometres. It identifies Canoe Creek and Canim Lake Bands using the document's historical wording. The service inventory (printed p.4 / PDF p.5) lists 100 Mile District General Hospital and Mill Site Lodge as funded and administered by the CHC. Other listed programs have different agency responsibilities, so the council should not be described as directly operating every local health service.

KMBR's own project record names the council as client for the South Cariboo Health Centre and describes connections between the hospital, health centre, and Mill Site Lodge.

## Reconstruction method

The two trees were rebuilt from deliberately authored branch tiers, central masses, detached twigs, and negative ink breaks. No automatic bitmap contour extraction or tracing was used. Branch shoulders use curves; broad proportions and the placement of visible voids were checked repeatedly against the supplied colour image. The pure-path master uses Boolean combinations of these authored shapes, giving real transparent openings rather than white paint. The separate construction SVG retains branch names and editable cuts.

The lettering was rebuilt from ITC Souvenir Demi letterforms in Monotype's public 2021 Type Trends specimen (page 8) and fitted to the four-line arrangement. It was the strongest tested visual match; this does not establish the historical font vendor or exact original file. Times, Georgia, Goudy and other serif candidates were compared and rejected as weaker matches. Detailed font evidence and fit parameters are recorded in the construction folder. The finished portable SVG uses paths and has no dependency on an installed typeface.

The sampled screen colours are dark green #417A3A and pale green #9BBC95. These are reconstruction values measured from the supplied JPEG-derived image; they are not documented official Pantone or print specifications.

## What remains uncertain

The exact historical font vendor/cut, logo designer, original adoption date, original print colours, and official symbolism of the trees remain unverified. The foliage has been deliberately redrawn from a low-resolution reference. It preserves the recognisable arrangement and major branch openings, but cannot recover every original brush mark. Tiny isolated marks are especially ambiguous. The pale tree's right side is partly obscured by the lettering and any hidden geometry is interpretive.

The opening-ceremony thank-you page mentions Cynthia Gardner Design among many contributors. It does not credit that business with designing the logo. It is an archival lead only. No tree species or symbolic explanation is assigned without evidence.

## Scope of the search

This task independently inspected the retained original health plan, its raster image, the two archived web logo variants, relevant supporting HTML, and the 1994 and 1997 legal orders. It also searched the council name, image filenames, design wording, and attribution lead. Earlier retained research examined 27 archived PDF URLs representing 26 unique PDFs and 149 unique pages without locating a vector emblem. That prior finding was reviewed, not repeated page by page during this task. A fresh archive-index request returned HTTP 503; the search should not be represented as exhaustive coverage of all historical material.

## Tree reconstruction reopened after visual review — 22 November 2026

The first refinement was insufficient in the trees. A second round rebuilt directional branch strokes, irregular openings, the lower hook, connected roots and detached marks. Repeated source landmarks support an approximately five-sixths shared branch construction for the pale tree, with independent right-side completion. This is an interpretation of source structure, not proof that an original vector was duplicated. See Refinement_Log.md and the current refinement report for comparisons and measurements.

The focused additional search did not recover a sharper specimen of the same brush drawing. The later Health Plan copy was byte-identical to the retained PDF; seventeen selected council-document pages contained no logo. An earlier annual-report link was found, but its PDF was unavailable and its contents could not be inspected. Full URLs, inspected material, retrieval limits and qualifications are recorded in Source_Search_Followup.md. The historical findings above remain unchanged.
