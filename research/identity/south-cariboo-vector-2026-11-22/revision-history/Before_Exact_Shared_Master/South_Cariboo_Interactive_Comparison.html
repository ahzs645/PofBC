What changed

The trees now share an authored branch master: the pale left structure uses approximately 5/6 scale, with a separately rebuilt right canopy. Directional brush strokes, interrupted tips, white channels and roots were reconstructed through successive overlay reviews. The original palette and lettering are preserved.

Where interpretation remains

Repeated branch landmarks support the shared construction, but do not prove that the historical artwork used one complete duplicated tree. The source is only 315 × 141 pixels; antialiasing, tiny marks and the pale portions hidden by lettering leave some shapes uncertain. Coloured fringes show differences in coverage, tone or shape.

How to read the comparison and measurements

Opacity overlay: 0% shows the original; 100% shows the selected reconstruction. The vector is composited against white so its transparent openings are compared correctly. Vertical wipe: original on the left, selected reconstruction on the right. Red / cyan: red indicates greater original ink density, cyan greater vector density, and neutral black or gray indicates similar density.

Each vector is rendered at 1890 × 846 and reduced to the original 315 × 141 grid with Lanczos for measurement. The density overlay is continuous, with no threshold. Foreground statistics use projected green-ink density ≥ 0.25 for all artwork and the pale window, and ≥ 0.75 for the dark tree and clear-background lettering. Boundary distances are symmetric; region-cut edges are excluded. No additional local warp was applied for this comparison.

The trees were rebuilt from deliberately authored branch shapes and negative openings, using repeated visible landmarks to place the pale instance. No automatic tracing, raster contour extraction or per-pixel path fitting was used. The name retains the fitted typeface curves from the previous version. The measurements describe agreement with the supplied raster; they do not establish an authentic historical master.

All artwork and controls are embedded. This comparison can be opened offline and requires no external fonts or services.
