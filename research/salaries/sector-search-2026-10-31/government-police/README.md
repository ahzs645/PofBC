# Government, police and regional-district source search

Audit date: **October 31, 2026, America/Vancouver**. [findings.json](findings.json) is the machine-readable inventory; it separates accepted data, located originals needing processing, archive navigation/retrieval and potential records acquisition. No requests or messages were sent to public bodies and no fees were accepted.

[work-queue.json](work-queue.json) normalizes 55 follow-up tasks: 30 processing, 13 public-archive research, 11 ordinary-records-request candidates and one Metro clarification. The police request candidates explicitly require checking open board/FOI archives first. No formal FOI has been established as the only remaining route. Regional-district task slugs are discovery identifiers and do not create app employer identities.

The accepted [Metro Vancouver employee collection](../../sector-government-police/README.md) adds **6,495 official observations for 2022–2025**, with exact per-source monetary residual warnings. Nine additional named rows are held because benefits/other is undisclosed; expense dashes remain missing. Partial coverage is explicit in the affected 2022/2024/2025 source warnings. Metro Vancouver remains outside the municipal denominator. Governing-board/committee observations and old compiled data remain separate.

All **27 regional districts** received an official-site discovery check. There are **16 downloaded SOFI sample originals**, with hashes and text/table diagnostics in `pdf-sample-retrievals.json`. Twelve have native text. Four are image-only and need OCR followed by complete row and control verification: **Bulkley-Nechako, North Coast, Okanagan-Similkameen and qathet**. This is a processing queue; their originals do not require procurement. Archive examples include Capital 2017–2025 and North Coast 2005–2025. Index labels are discovery evidence, not proof of a document's internal reporting year. Eleven other districts still require deeper financial-report/board-archive navigation or retrieval; their reports are not declared missing. Thompson-Nicola returned HTTP403 through direct site retrieval, but official CDN SOFI originals and the final-report availability notice were located.

All **13 compiled police employers** have an entry. City SOFI coverage does not establish police coverage: official sources explicitly exclude police for several municipalities. Victoria's 2021 SOFI explains the provincial instruction to exclude municipal police-board employee remuneration. Each police-origin report still needs its own publication and redaction review. Salary rates, pay-transparency gender-gap reports and police-board honoraria do not establish named workforce actual pay.

Vancouver Police's published FOI archive contains staff releases labeled 2007, 2008, 2009, 2012 and 2013, plus an executive series for 2014–2025. The downloaded 2009 and 2012 staff PDFs have no native text and need OCR. The native 2013 file contains many unnamed constable rows: published redactions must be preserved, and names must never be reconstructed. Its archive year and original reporting period need confirmation together. Later executive releases must remain executive-only. These existing releases are processing candidates before any request for missing years.

Transit Police is available as a separate subsidiary schedule within official TransLink filings; the crown-sector agent inspected the 2025 employee pages 214–220 and holds the 2024/2025 filing inventory. Existing `metro-vancouver-transit-police` and `transit-police` compiled groups overlap in 2017/18, with different row counts. Neither deletion nor identity consolidation is justified by the shared branding alone.

Two machine-readable provincial histories were recovered in DataBC: **Members of the Legislative Assembly Compensation** (19 indexed resources: FYE2007 and FYE2009–2026) and **Premier and Ministers Salary and Travel Expenses** (20 resources). Exact CSV links and latest/earliest sample hashes/headers are retained. Repeated MLA rows represent explicit office/committee allowances; ministerial salary can supplement member compensation. Living allowance and travel are separate payment categories. These need a deliberate component and overlap model before app integration, rather than creating multiple annual salary observations for a single member. Missing MLA FYE2008 should first be recovered from the original Public Accounts schedule.

For core government before 2006/07, an exact 2005/06 CRF detailed-schedules PDF was located, but direct retrieval returned HTTP503 in this pass. Retry the official PDF/browser/archive route before treating it as procurement. The existing Legislative Library archive supplies historical scanned volumes, including nineteenth-century material through 1984/85; large scans require page selection and OCR, not automatic whole-volume salary imports. Judiciary work remains source discovery: actual paid disclosures must be distinguished from statutory salary rates and from federal superior-court remuneration.

Potential acquisition work is explicit per employer in `findings.json`: first search open board/FOI archives; then ask the appropriate police/finance/archive custodian for existing published or releasable remuneration schedules and native spreadsheets. Formal FOI is a fallback for specific unavailable originals. Existing provincial requests for 1998/99, 1999/2000 and accessible 2001/02 originals remain relevant. A failed HTTP retrieval or a navigation gap is not proof that an FOI application is necessary.

`retrieval-evidence.json` pins requested/final official URLs, HTTP outcomes, page hashes and discovered links for both navigation passes. Those are index fingerprints, not source acceptance. `catalogue-sample-retrievals.json` retains raw-source hashes and sample schemas. Original PDFs remain in `/tmp/pofbc-sector-government-police` for independent reproduction and are intentionally not committed as large binaries.
