# Additional official council-pay observations

Retrieved 4 November 2026. This note documents `official-additional-timeseries.json` only.

## Coverage and definitions

The file contains **142 observations from 11 sources across 18 municipalities**: 28 annual-rate observations and 114 comparative survey observations. Both Mayor and Councillor are represented for every municipality/year listed below. These are selected observations, not a complete province-wide annual series.

Abbotsford has sourced rate coverage for 2007–2015. The 2007–November 2011 observations are calendar-year slices of one explicitly dated interval in the January 2011 review, not independently observed annual payroll schedules. A separate adopted rate began on 1 December 2011; 2011 therefore has two successive rates, neither of which is a full-year payment. The other sources provide snapshots and selected own-city rates. No missing years are interpolated, no rates are derived from CPI, and no proposed increases are recorded as implemented pay.

## Qualifications and unresolved discrepancies

- **Abbotsford:** the historical one-third tax-free component is already within the listed remuneration; it is not added as extra salary. The 2007–November 2011 interval is reported in the original January 2011 review, whose methodology says figures are rounded. The new interval rows retain the displayed $100 precision. The later policy describes its one-third component as an incidental-expense allowance within remuneration. The larger report package contains proposed 2016/2017 figures; those were excluded. The table explicitly documents the unchanged implemented amounts for 2013–2015.
- **Survey precision:** the 2023 Victoria-commissioned survey prints amounts in thousands to one decimal place. CAD conversion preserves $100 precision in both notes and `amountPrecisionCad`. Other comparative tables are transcribed at their published whole-dollar precision, without invented cents.
- **Acting-mayor amounts:** the 2018 and 2025 Nanaimo surveys provide separate acting-pay estimates and caution that some service periods are assumed. Each relevant councillor row retains that amount in notes, separately from the office-pay amount. Those conditional estimates are not exported as unconditional annual supplements. North Vancouver City’s $1,634/month and Victoria’s $1,750/month are also kept separate.
- **Nanaimo:** the 2015, 2017 and 2018 surveys print councillor pay of $35,447. The existing March 2016 city announcement prints $35,438. The December 2018 report says base salaries had not changed since 2015, so the small disagreement is unresolved and neither figure is silently substituted. The 2025 survey’s own-city row is preserved exactly; later budget/proration scenarios are excluded.
- **Victoria:** the 2017 Prince George survey’s blanket tax-free statement conflicts with Victoria’s own 2022 governance review and the 2018 Nanaimo survey, which say the city had used fully taxable remuneration since 2009. This does not trigger an adjustment to any recorded amount. The 2024 adopted bylaw sets $133,147/$53,259, a separate one-time $1,750 acting-mayor indemnity, and later monthly acting pay. A personal councillor income table with different base amounts was not generalized into office rates.
- **North Vancouver:** City and District are separate employers and labels. Victoria’s 2022 comparison table is excluded because it labels North Vancouver as the District while giving the City’s published rates. Its older, explicitly unverified Kamloops figures are also excluded.
- **Remaining coverage:** this file adds only the 2017 Prince George survey pair and 2015 Richmond and Surrey pairs. It does not establish continuous histories for those municipalities or Burnaby. Nanaimo has large gaps before 2015 and after 2018. Apparent consecutive coverage assembled from CBC, surveys and adopted schedules must not be presented as one uniform base-rate series.

## Extraction and validation

Figures were transcribed from indexed text of official city publications, with table labels and surrounding report context inspected. The 2018 Nanaimo attachment was cross-checked in the city’s released 2019 Q4 collection at printed page 149; the standalone attachment URL redirected to the accompanying report when opened. No original-file byte hash or original PDF cache is claimed. The added Abbotsford 2007–2011 interval was also verified visually on PDF page 35; no visual verification is claimed for the other tables in this file.

Validation used the existing `validateDataset` function on the new file and on the new observations merged with the current companion dataset. Both passed. Additional checks confirmed 11 unique source IDs, 142 unique observation IDs, source references, valid amounts/years/measures, and all 18 employer slugs against the municipal roster. The file contains no named payments. The interval extension preserved all 132 earlier observations and 10 earlier sources unchanged, including the corrected Victoria 2022 page-26 locators. The builder and generated outputs were not changed by this research task.
