# Province of British Columbia: engraved arms, before 1977 (reconstruction)

**A historical reconstruction for reference and study. This is not the Province’s current
identity**, which the Government of British Columbia publishes.

Before the Visual Identity Program of 1977 gave the Province one symbol (the crest and ring) and
one typeface (Helvetica), a minister’s stationery carried the full coat of arms, engraved, over
formal script. This kit is that era’s first piece of evidence turned into a brand: the calling card
of Grace M. McCarthy, Deputy Premier, Provincial Secretary and Minister of Recreation & Travel
Industry.

## What is here

- **The arms** (`artwork/arms.svg`): the supplied coat-of-arms engraving, its path preserved
  exactly (`fill-rule="evenodd"`), as the brand’s symbol and its one signature, in olive-gold,
  black and white.
- **The card** (Templates → Calling cards): the reconstruction’s 1800 × 1038 artboard with all
  seven lines as fields (name, three title lines, telephone, city and province), so any minister’s
  card of the period can be set. The photographed card is attached as the studio’s reference
  overlay.
- **Three script faces**: PlateForge Card Script Display Study (name), Compact Study (titles) and
  Card Script Study (telephone, city, province), source-informed studies derived from the open
  Pinyon Script, each with its SIL Open Font License and provenance (`fonts/`).
- **The offline card editor** (`docs/card-editor.html`), which needs no server or network.
- **The evidence** (`reference/`): the original photograph, its perspective-corrected version, the
  reconstruction preview, font comparison and overlay diagnostics, and the measurement and
  validation records. `docs/RECONSTRUCTION.md` is the reconstruction’s own report. Its links
  describe the original package; its `model.json` (the font bytes again, 5 MB) is left out here.

## What is not established

- The historical typeface: formal engraved script, consistent with Commercial Script, but not
  identified. The three study cuts are fitting choices, not evidence of three typefaces.
- The arms: the supplied engraving is a different drawing from the card’s (its ribbon and
  supporters differ), kept at its own proportions (about 0.889) rather than stretched to the
  card’s (about 1.515).
- The card’s date, printed size and ink colours. The olive-gold is a visual approximation.
- When this era ended. The card’s titles date it to Grace McCarthy’s ministry (from late 1975),
  and the Visual Identity Program followed in 1977; how long engraved arms stayed on ministerial
  stationery is not yet known.

## Rebuilding

This folder is generated by PofBC (`npm run brandkit:build -- bc-province-engraved-arms`) from
`brandkit/sources/bc-province-engraved-arms/` and `brandkit/scripts/eras/engraved-arms.mjs`;
`npm run brandkit:pack` makes it a brand pack. Add the era’s next piece (a letterhead, an
envelope, another card) as a template there.
